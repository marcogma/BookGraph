# Definition

Nietzsche's distinction between morality created by strong, self-affirming individuals (master morality) and morality born from resentment — the weak inverting the natural order by making weakness itself virtuous (slave morality). Applied broadly: Nietzsche reads Kant's categorical imperative, Stoic self-control, and the French Revolution's egalitarianism all as versions of slave morality dressed up as universal reason. His alternative isn't cruelty but self-overcoming — the three metamorphoses from camel (bearing inherited burdens) to lion (saying "no" to them) to child (creating new values freely).

I pushed back on part of this: Nietzsche treats self-control and virtue as suppression that limits potential, but I think discipline can just as easily be mastery rather than denial — someone ruled by anger or fear is a slave to those impulses, so real self-control can be what frees you to act deliberately rather than reactively. That doesn't fully resolve the disagreement, but it's a real crack in the framework worth holding onto rather than accepting Nietzsche's read of Stoicism wholesale.
