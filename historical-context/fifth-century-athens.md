# What happened

After defeating Persia (490 and 480–479 BCE), Athens became a naval empire and a direct democracy in which adult male citizens voted in the Assembly. Under Pericles it built the Parthenon and funded drama. Tragedies were performed at the City Dionysia each spring as a competition: three playwrights each presented three tragedies and a satyr play before thousands of citizens.

Sophocles (c. 497–406 BCE) wrote about 120 plays, of which seven survive, and won the competition more often than anyone. He also held public office, serving as a general alongside Pericles in 441–440. The Peloponnesian War against Sparta (431–404) and the plague of 430–426, which killed Pericles, ended in Athens' defeat and a short pro-Spartan oligarchy, the Thirty Tyrants (404–403), which included relatives of Plato. In 399 the restored democracy tried and executed Socrates for impiety and corrupting the young. Plato (c. 428–348), his student, wrote dialogues with Socrates as the main speaker and founded the Academy around 387.
