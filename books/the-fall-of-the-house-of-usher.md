# Summary

A collection of Poe's gothic tales; contents vary by edition, usually with "Ligeia", "The Masque of the Red Death", "The Pit and the Pendulum", and "William Wilson". In the title story (1839), the narrator visits his childhood friend Roderick Usher, who lives with his twin sister Madeline in a decaying mansion with a crack running down its front. Roderick suffers from extreme sensitivity of the senses and fear; Madeline has a wasting illness and trances. She seems to die, and the two men place her body in a vault inside the house. Over the next days Roderick grows more agitated. During a storm, while the narrator reads him a story aloud, sounds in the house echo the story. Roderick screams that they buried her alive and that he heard her for days without daring to speak. Madeline appears at the door, bloody, and falls on her brother, and both die. The narrator flees, and the house splits along the crack and sinks into the lake.

# Main characters

- **Roderick Usher**: the last male of the family
- **Madeline Usher**: his twin sister, buried alive
- **The narrator**: Roderick's childhood friend

# Historical & cultural context

See [[poe-and-the-invention-of-detective-fiction]], which covers Poe's life. "The Fall of the House of Usher" first appeared in *Burton's Gentleman's Magazine* in 1839. Fear of premature burial was common in the 19th century, and Poe returned to it in "The Premature Burial" (1844).
