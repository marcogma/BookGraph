# Summary

Meursault, a clerk in Algiers, narrates. "Aujourd'hui, maman est morte." He goes to his mother's funeral at the old people's home and does not cry. The next day he swims with Marie, a former office colleague, sees a comedy film, and starts an affair with her. When she asks if he loves her, he says it doesn't mean anything but probably not. He helps his neighbor Raymond, a pimp, write a letter to trick his Arab mistress, whom Raymond then beats. On a beach, after a fight between Raymond's group and the woman's brother and his friends, Meursault returns alone in the blinding sun and shoots one of the Arabs once, then four more times. In the second part, in prison and at trial, the prosecutor focuses less on the killing than on his not crying at the funeral, drinking coffee by the coffin, and seeing a comedy the next day. He is sentenced to be guillotined. A chaplain visits him; Meursault explodes in anger. Afterward he feels at peace, opens himself to "the tender indifference of the world", and hopes for a crowd at his execution greeting him with cries of hate.

# Main characters

- **Meursault**: the narrator, a clerk who will not pretend to feel what he doesn't
- **Marie Cardona**: his girlfriend
- **Raymond Sintès**: his neighbor, whose quarrel leads to the killing
- **Salamano**: an old neighbor who beats his dog and grieves when it runs away
- **Masson**: Raymond's friend, who owns the beach house
- **The chaplain**: who tries to make Meursault turn to God

# Historical & cultural context

See [[french-existentialism]], which also covers Camus's Algiers childhood. Published in 1942, in the same year as *Le Mythe de Sisyphe*. The murdered man is never named. The Algerian writer Kamel Daoud's novel *Meursault, contre-enquête* (2013) retells the story from the point of view of the dead man's brother, who gives him a name, Musa.
