# Summary

Nine linked stories, framed as a reporter in the 21st century interviewing the robot psychologist Dr. Susan Calvin at the end of her career at U.S. Robots and Mechanical Men. All robots follow the Three Laws of Robotics: a robot may not harm a human or allow harm through inaction; it must obey humans unless that conflicts with the First Law; it must protect itself unless that conflicts with the first two. Most stories are puzzles about robots behaving strangely because of how the laws interact. "Robbie": a mute nursemaid robot is taken from the girl who loves it. "Runaround": on Mercury, a robot circles a selenium pool, stuck between two laws, until the engineers Powell and Donovan risk their lives to break the loop. "Reason": a robot decides humans can't have made it and starts its own religion, while still doing its job. "Liar!": a mind-reading robot tells people what they want to hear to avoid hurting them, and Calvin destroys it with a paradox. "Little Lost Robot": a robot with a weakened First Law hides among identical ones. "Evidence": a politician, Stephen Byerley, may be a robot, and nobody can prove it. "The Evitable Conflict": giant Machines now run the world economy and are quietly steering humanity for its own good.

# Main characters

- **Susan Calvin**: the robopsychologist who tells the stories
- **Gregory Powell and Mike Donovan**: the field engineers who test robots
- **Robbie**: the nursemaid robot
- **Stephen Byerley**: the politician who may be a robot
- **The Three Laws of Robotics**: the rules built into every robot's brain

# Historical & cultural context

The stories first appeared in science fiction magazines from 1940 to 1950, most in John W. Campbell's *Astounding*, and were collected in 1950. Asimov (1920–1992) was born in Russia, grew up in Brooklyn, and was also a biochemistry professor. The Three Laws were first stated in full in "Runaround" (1942); Asimov credited Campbell with helping formulate them. The word "robotics" appears in that story, one of its earliest uses in print.
