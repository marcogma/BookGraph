# Summary

A narrator returns, twenty-seven years later, to a Caribbean river town to reconstruct the killing of his friend Santiago Nasar. The rich outsider Bayardo San Román married Ángela Vicario in a huge wedding, then returned her to her family the same night because she was not a virgin. Pressed by her family, she named Santiago Nasar. Her twin brothers, Pedro and Pablo Vicario, announced to almost the whole town that they would kill him to restore the family's honor, half hoping someone would stop them. Through bad luck, indifference, and people assuming someone else would warn him, nobody does, and the twins stab him to death at his own front door, which his mother had just closed. The book never settles whether Santiago was guilty. Ángela spends years writing letters to Bayardo, who finally comes back to her carrying all of them, unopened.

# Main characters

- **Santiago Nasar**: the young man of Arab descent who is killed
- **Ángela Vicario**: the bride returned on her wedding night, who names Santiago
- **Bayardo San Román**: the wealthy stranger who marries and rejects her
- **Pedro and Pablo Vicario**: the twin pig butchers who kill Santiago
- **Plácida Linero**: Santiago's mother, who bars the door without knowing
- **The narrator**: an unnamed friend of Santiago's, close to García Márquez himself

# Historical & cultural context

See [[gabriel-garcia-marquez-aracataca-and-journalism]]. The novella (1981) is based on the 1951 killing of García Márquez's friend Cayetano Gentile Chimento in Sucre, Colombia; his mother asked him not to write about it while the people involved were alive. It came out a year before he won the Nobel Prize and uses the methods of a journalistic investigation: interviews, the court record, conflicting testimony.
