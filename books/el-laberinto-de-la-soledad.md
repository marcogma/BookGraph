# Summary

A book of essays on Mexican identity. Paz starts with the pachuco, the young Mexican-American in Los Angeles who is neither Mexican nor American. He describes the Mexican as closed and guarded behind masks, explosive in fiestas, and familiar with death (the Day of the Dead) while refusing to face it. In "Los hijos de la Malinche" he discusses the verb *chingar* and the figure of La Malinche, Cortés's Indigenous interpreter and mistress, as the "violated mother" from whom Mexicans feel they descend. He then goes through Mexican history: the Conquest, the colony as a closed order, Independence and the Reform as imported ideas that did not fit, and the Revolution as Mexico's attempt to find itself. The final essay, "La dialéctica de la soledad", argues that solitude is a human condition and not only a Mexican one. The 1970 *Posdata* responds to the Tlatelolco massacre.

# Main characters

- **The pachuco**: the Mexican-American youth in Los Angeles who opens the book
- **La Malinche**: Cortés's interpreter, the 'chingada' mother of the essay
- **Máscaras**: Paz's term for Mexican reserve and self-defense
- **La fiesta**: the explosion that breaks that reserve

# Historical & cultural context

See [[the-pri-era-mexico-1940-1982]] (it was written in 1950, under Alemán, and Paz added *Posdata* after Tlatelolco) and [[the-mexican-revolution-and-the-cristero-war]].
