# Summary

Part One (1605): Alonso Quijano, a poor gentleman in La Mancha of about fifty, reads so many books of chivalry that he loses his mind. He calls himself Don Quixote, puts on old armor, takes his thin horse Rocinante, and chooses a peasant girl he has barely seen as his lady, Dulcinea del Toboso. He persuades the farmer Sancho Panza to be his squire by promising him an island to govern. He takes inns for castles and windmills for giants, frees galley slaves who then stone him, and fights flocks of sheep. Many other stories are mixed in. His friends, the priest and the barber, burn his books and finally bring him home in a cage. Part Two (1615): the two heroes are now famous because Part One has been published, and people they meet have read it. A duke and duchess invite them to their castle only to play cruel jokes on them; Sancho is given a fake "island" to govern and does it wisely. They also run into readers of the fake sequel by Avellaneda. In Barcelona, the Knight of the White Moon (the bachelor Sansón Carrasco in disguise) defeats Don Quixote and makes him promise to go home for a year. Back home, he falls ill, regains his sanity, renounces chivalry, makes his will as Alonso Quijano "the Good", and dies, while Sancho begs him to go out again.

# Main characters

- **Don Quixote (Alonso Quijano)**: the gentleman who thinks he is a knight-errant
- **Sancho Panza**: his squire, a practical peasant
- **Dulcinea del Toboso**: the imagined lady, really the farm girl Aldonza Lorenzo
- **Rocinante**: his horse
- **The priest and the barber**: his friends who try to bring him home
- **Sansón Carrasco**: the bachelor who finally defeats him as the Knight of the White Moon
- **The Duke and Duchess**: aristocrats who make fun of him in Part Two
- **Ginés de Pasamonte**: a galley slave who reappears as a puppet master
- **Cide Hamete Benengeli**: the fictional Arab historian the narrator says wrote the story
