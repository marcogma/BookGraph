# Summary

On Manor Farm, the old boar Old Major tells the animals of a dream in which they are free of humans, teaches them the song "Beasts of England", and dies. Soon after, the animals drive out the drunken farmer Mr. Jones and rename the place Animal Farm. The pigs, the cleverest animals, take charge and write Seven Commandments on the barn wall, ending with "All animals are equal". Two pigs compete for power: Snowball, full of plans for a windmill, and Napoleon, who quietly raises a litter of puppies as his private guard. Napoleon uses the dogs to drive Snowball away, then blames him for every failure. The pigs move into the farmhouse, sleep in beds, drink alcohol, and trade with humans, and their spokesman Squealer explains each change while the commandments on the wall are quietly altered. Animals who confess to plotting are executed. Boxer, the loyal horse whose motto is "I will work harder", collapses and is sold to a knacker for slaughter, while Squealer claims he died in hospital. At the end the only commandment left is "All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others". The pigs walk on two legs and play cards with the neighboring farmers, and the animals outside the window can no longer tell pig from man.

# Main characters

- **Old Major**: the old boar whose dream starts the revolution (Marx and Lenin)
- **Napoleon**: the pig who becomes dictator (Stalin)
- **Snowball**: his rival, expelled and blamed for everything (Trotsky)
- **Squealer**: the pig who justifies every change (propaganda)
- **Boxer**: the loyal, hard-working cart horse
- **Benjamin**: the cynical old donkey
- **Mollie**: the vain mare who leaves for ribbons and sugar
- **Moses**: the raven who talks of Sugarcandy Mountain (religion)
- **Mr. Jones**: the farmer who is driven out
- **Mr. Pilkington and Mr. Frederick**: the neighboring farmers
