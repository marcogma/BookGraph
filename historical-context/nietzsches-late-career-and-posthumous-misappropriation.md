# What happened

By the early 1880s Nietzsche had broken with both Wagner and Schopenhauer's pessimism, resigned his Basel professorship for health reasons (severe migraines and eye pain), and entered a period of solitary wandering between Sils-Maria, Nice, and Turin in which he wrote his major late works at a rapid pace — *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* (1883–85) first, then *Beyond Good and Evil* (1886) explicitly as a more direct, aphoristic companion to clarify what *Zarathustra* had said in parable form. In January 1889, in Turin, Nietzsche suffered a complete mental and physical collapse from which he never recovered, living his final eleven years in the care of his mother and then his sister, Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche, until his death in 1900.

Elisabeth matters in her own right here. Years earlier she had co-founded a racially "pure" German colony in Paraguay (Nueva Germania) with her antisemitic husband Bernhard Förster — views Nietzsche despised and mocked explicitly in his own letters. After his collapse she took control of his literary estate, and after his death compiled his unpublished notebook fragments into *The Will to Power* (1901), heavily edited and reordered to fit her own nationalist, antisemitic reading — a book Nietzsche never wrote or approved in that form. She later courted Hitler directly, who visited her archive and funded it. This is the direct root of Nietzsche's long, and largely false, association with Nazism — a reading modern scholarship treats as a distortion of the actual texts, not a description of them.

# Grounds these books

- [[beyond-good-and-evil]] (1886) — written as the clarifying companion to *Zarathustra*, from the same isolated, declining-health period
- [[thus-spoke-zarathustra]] (1883–85) — the text most directly mined and distorted by Elisabeth's later editing, since its poetic, prophetic form is the easiest of Nietzsche's books to misread out of context
