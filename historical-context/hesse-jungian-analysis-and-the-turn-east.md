# What happened

Hermann Hesse (1877–1962) was born in Calw, in Württemberg, into a family of Pietist Protestant missionaries. His mother was born in India, and his grandfather Hermann Gundert had been a missionary there and wrote a Malayalam dictionary. Hesse ran away from the Maulbronn seminary at fourteen. In 1911 he traveled to Ceylon, Malaya, and Sumatra, a trip that disappointed him.

When the First World War began he published an essay asking German intellectuals not to join in nationalist hatred ("O Freunde, nicht diese Töne", 1914), and the German press attacked him as a traitor. Between that, his father's death, his son's illness, and his wife's mental illness, he broke down in 1916 and went into analysis with Josef Bernhard Lang, a student of Carl Jung; in 1921 he also had sessions with Jung himself. *Demian* (1919) came out of that analysis, and *Siddhartha* (1922) out of his family's ties to India. He became a Swiss citizen in 1924 and won the Nobel Prize in 1946. In the 1960s his books became popular with the American counterculture; the rock band Steppenwolf took its name from his novel.
