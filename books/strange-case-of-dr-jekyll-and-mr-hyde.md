# Summary

The edition I logged includes other stories (contents vary); this summary covers the title novella. The London lawyer Utterson hears from his cousin Enfield of a small, repellent man, Edward Hyde, who trampled a little girl and paid compensation with a check signed by Utterson's respected friend Dr. Henry Jekyll. Jekyll's will leaves everything to Hyde. A year later Hyde beats an old member of Parliament, Sir Danvers Carew, to death with a cane, and disappears. Jekyll seems to recover and becomes sociable, then shuts himself away. Dr. Lanyon, Jekyll's old colleague, dies of shock after something he saw. Finally Jekyll's butler Poole fetches Utterson because a strange voice is speaking from Jekyll's locked laboratory. They break in and find Hyde dead, a suicide, wearing Jekyll's clothes. Two documents explain everything. Lanyon's letter tells how he watched Hyde drink a potion and turn into Jekyll. Jekyll's statement tells how he created a drug to separate his evil side, enjoyed being Hyde, started turning into Hyde without the drug, and ran out of the salt that made the potion work, since the original batch had an unknown impurity.

# Main characters

- **Dr. Henry Jekyll**: the respected doctor
- **Edward Hyde**: his evil other self
- **Gabriel Utterson**: the lawyer who investigates
- **Dr. Hastie Lanyon**: Jekyll's former friend who sees the transformation
- **Richard Enfield**: Utterson's cousin, who first tells the story of Hyde
- **Poole**: Jekyll's butler
- **Sir Danvers Carew**: the MP Hyde murders

# Historical & cultural context

See [[victorian-fin-de-siecle-gothic]]. Stevenson (1850–1894) wrote it in a few days in 1885 in Bournemouth, while ill. According to his wife Fanny, he burned the first draft after she criticized it and rewrote it. It came out in January 1886 and sold 40,000 copies in six months in Britain. Stevenson grew up in Edinburgh, where the respectable cabinetmaker and city councillor William Brodie was hanged in 1788 for leading a gang of burglars at night; Stevenson had co-written a play about him.
