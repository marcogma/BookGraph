# Summary

The edition I logged gathers *The Metamorphosis* with other stories (contents vary by edition; usually including "The Judgment", "In the Penal Colony", and "A Hunger Artist"). In *The Metamorphosis* (1915) the traveling salesman Gregor Samsa wakes up turned into a huge insect. His first worry is missing his train; he has been supporting his parents and sister and paying his father's debts. His family is horrified and locks him in his room. His sister Grete feeds him at first. His father wounds him by throwing an apple that lodges in his back and rots. The family takes jobs and rents rooms to three lodgers. When Gregor comes out to hear Grete play the violin, the lodgers see him and give notice, and Grete says they must get rid of "it". Gregor dies that night. The charwoman throws him out, and the family, relieved, takes a tram into the country and notices that Grete has become a young woman ready for marriage. In "The Judgment" (1912) Georg Bendemann's father condemns him to death by drowning, and Georg runs and jumps from a bridge. In "In the Penal Colony" (1919) an officer shows a visiting explorer a machine that writes a prisoner's sentence into his skin until he dies. When the explorer disapproves, the officer frees the prisoner and puts himself in the machine, which breaks down and kills him. In "A Hunger Artist" (1922) a man who fasts in a cage for audiences loses his public and starves himself to death, saying he only fasted because he never found food he liked.

# Main characters

- **Gregor Samsa**: the salesman who becomes an insect
- **Grete Samsa**: his younger sister
- **Mr. and Mrs. Samsa**: his parents
- **The chief clerk**: who comes from Gregor's office when he misses his train
- **The three lodgers**: the boarders who leave when they see Gregor
- **Georg Bendemann**: the son in "The Judgment"
- **The officer and the explorer**: in "In the Penal Colony"

# Historical & cultural context

See [[kafkas-bureaucratic-prague-and-posthumous-publication]]. Unlike *The Trial* and *The Castle*, these stories were published in Kafka's lifetime. *The Metamorphosis* came out in 1915 from Kurt Wolff; Kafka asked the publisher not to draw the insect on the cover. Kafka wrote "The Judgment" in one night in September 1912 and later said it was the way to write.
