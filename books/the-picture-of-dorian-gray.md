# Summary

The painter Basil Hallward paints a portrait of the beautiful young Dorian Gray. Basil's friend Lord Henry Wotton tells Dorian that youth and beauty are all that matter, and Dorian wishes that the picture would grow old instead of him. He falls in love with Sibyl Vane, a young actress who plays Shakespeare in a poor theater. When she acts badly because she is now in love with him in real life, he cruelly rejects her, and she kills herself. Dorian notices that the face in the portrait has changed, with a touch of cruelty. He hides it in the locked schoolroom of his house. Influenced by a "poisonous" French book Lord Henry gives him, he lives for pleasure for eighteen years, staying young while his reputation darkens and people around him are ruined. The portrait grows old and ugly. When Basil asks about the rumors, Dorian shows him the picture and then stabs him to death. He blackmails the chemist Alan Campbell into destroying the body; Campbell later kills himself. Sibyl's brother James tries to kill Dorian and is accidentally shot in a hunt. Finally Dorian stabs the portrait with the same knife. His servants find the portrait young and beautiful again, and on the floor an old, withered man with a knife in his heart, recognizable only by his rings.

# Main characters

- **Dorian Gray**: the young man whose portrait ages instead of him
- **Lord Henry Wotton**: the witty aristocrat whose ideas influence him
- **Basil Hallward**: the painter who loves Dorian's beauty
- **Sibyl Vane**: the actress who kills herself
- **James Vane**: her brother, who wants revenge
- **Alan Campbell**: the chemist Dorian blackmails

# Historical & cultural context

See [[victorian-fin-de-siecle-gothic]]. First published in *Lippincott's Monthly Magazine* in 1890; British reviewers called it immoral, and Wilde added a preface of aphorisms ("All art is quite useless") and six new chapters for the 1891 book. The "poisonous book" is generally identified with J.-K. Huysmans's *À rebours* (1884). Wilde (1854–1900) was convicted of "gross indecency" in 1895, served two years of hard labor, and died in Paris, poor, three years after his release.
