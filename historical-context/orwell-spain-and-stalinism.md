# What happened

George Orwell (Eric Blair, 1903–1950) went to Spain in December 1936 to fight against Franco. He joined the militia of the POUM, an anti-Stalinist Marxist party, and was shot through the throat by a sniper in May 1937. That spring the Spanish Communists, backed by the Soviet Union, turned on the POUM: its leaders were arrested, Andreu Nin was killed, and Orwell and his wife escaped Spain while being hunted by the police. He described it in *Homage to Catalonia* (1938). Watching the Communist press call his comrades fascist agents, while the Moscow show trials were going on, convinced him that Stalinism was as dangerous as fascism and that the British left was lying about it.

*Animal Farm* was finished in 1944, when the USSR was Britain's ally; several publishers rejected it, including Faber, where T. S. Eliot turned it down. It came out in August 1945. Orwell wrote *Nineteen Eighty-Four* on the Scottish island of Jura while seriously ill with tuberculosis. It was published in June 1949 and he died in London in January 1950.

# Grounds these books

- [[animal-farm]]: an allegory of the Russian Revolution and Stalin's rise, written during the wartime alliance
- [[nineteen-eighty-four]]: the Party's lies and purges draw on what he saw of Communist propaganda in Spain
