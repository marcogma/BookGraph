# Summary

In colonial Cartagena in the 18th century, Sierva María, the neglected twelve-year-old daughter of the weak Marquis de Casalduero, has been raised by her family's enslaved Africans and speaks their languages. A rabid dog bites her. She shows no symptoms, but her strange behavior leads the Bishop to declare her possessed, and she is locked in the convent of Santa Clara. The young priest Cayetano Delaura, sent to exorcise her, falls in love with her, and she with him. He visits her secretly through a tunnel. When it is discovered, he is punished and sent to work in a leper hospital. The exorcisms continue, with her head shaved, and Sierva María dies in her cell, "of love", the morning of the next session, with her hair growing back fast. A prologue says the story began in 1949, when García Márquez as a young reporter watched workers empty the crypts of the convent and found the skull of a girl with twenty-two meters of copper hair.

# Main characters

- **Sierva María de Todos los Ángeles**: the marquis's daughter, declared possessed
- **Cayetano Delaura**: the young priest assigned to exorcise her, who falls in love with her
- **The Marquis de Casalduero**: her ineffective father
- **Bernarda Cabrera**: her mother, addicted to cacao and fermented honey
- **Abrenuncio**: a Portuguese Jewish doctor who thinks she is not ill
- **Dominga de Adviento**: the enslaved woman who raised her
- **The Bishop**: Don Toribio de Cáceres y Virtudes, who orders the exorcism

# Historical & cultural context

See [[gabriel-garcia-marquez-aracataca-and-journalism]]. Published in 1994. Cartagena was a main port of the Spanish slave trade and a seat of the Inquisition in the Americas (from 1610). Belief in demonic possession and the use of exorcism were part of colonial Church practice.
