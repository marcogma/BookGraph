# What happened

René Descartes (1596–1650) and Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677) are the two main continental rationalists: philosophers who tried to build knowledge from clear reasoning, on the model of geometry, instead of from tradition or the senses. Both worked in the Dutch Republic, the most tolerant country in Europe at the time.

Descartes, a Frenchman, lived in the Netherlands for about twenty years from 1628. When he heard of the Church's condemnation of Galileo in 1633 he withheld his own treatise on physics, *Le Monde*. He published the *Meditations* in Latin in 1641, together with objections from Thomas Hobbes, Antoine Arnauld, Pierre Gassendi, and others, plus his replies. He went to Stockholm in 1649 to teach Queen Christina and died there of pneumonia in 1650. Spinoza started from Descartes' work. The only book he published under his own name during his life was an exposition of Descartes' *Principles of Philosophy* (1663), and his *Ethics* uses a similar geometric method to reach very different conclusions about God and mind.
