# Summary

Dr. Mortimer comes to Sherlock Holmes in London with an old family legend: the wicked Hugo Baskerville was killed centuries ago by a huge spectral hound on Dartmoor, and the family has been cursed since. Sir Charles Baskerville has just been found dead on his estate, apparently of a heart attack, with the footprints of a gigantic hound nearby. The heir, Sir Henry, arrives from Canada and receives an anonymous warning. Holmes sends Dr. Watson to stay with him at Baskerville Hall. Watson investigates the butler Barrymore (whose wife is secretly feeding her brother, the escaped convict Selden), the naturalist Jack Stapleton and his "sister" Beryl, and Laura Lyons, a woman Sir Charles was helping. Holmes has been secretly camping on the moor. Selden, wearing Sir Henry's old clothes, is killed fleeing the hound. Stapleton is actually Rodger Baskerville's son, next in line to inherit, and Beryl is his wife. He keeps a large dog painted with phosphorus to look ghostly. Holmes uses Sir Henry as bait, shoots the hound as it attacks him, and Stapleton flees into the Grimpen Mire and is presumably swallowed by it.

# Main characters

- **Sherlock Holmes**: the detective
- **Dr. John Watson**: his friend, who narrates and does most of the fieldwork
- **Sir Henry Baskerville**: the heir in danger
- **Sir Charles Baskerville**: the uncle found dead
- **Dr. James Mortimer**: the local doctor who brings the case
- **Jack Stapleton**: the naturalist, really a Baskerville and the killer
- **Beryl Stapleton**: his wife, passed off as his sister
- **Mr. and Mrs. Barrymore**: the servants at Baskerville Hall
- **Selden**: the escaped convict
- **Laura Lyons**: the woman Stapleton uses to lure Sir Charles out

# Historical & cultural context

See [[victorian-fin-de-siecle-gothic]] and [[poe-and-the-invention-of-detective-fiction]]. Doyle had killed off Holmes at the Reichenbach Falls in 1893. *The Hound* was serialized in *The Strand* in 1901–02 as an earlier case, set before Holmes's "death"; Holmes was properly brought back in "The Adventure of the Empty House" (1903). Doyle got the legend from his friend Bertram Fletcher Robinson, who told him Dartmoor stories of a black hound.
