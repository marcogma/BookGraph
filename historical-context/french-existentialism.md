# What happened

Jean-Paul Sartre (1905–1980) published *La Nausée* in 1938. Drafted in 1939, he was taken prisoner by the Germans in 1940 and released in 1941. Under the Occupation he published *L'Être et le néant* (1943) and staged *Les Mouches* (1943) and *Huis clos* (1944), both passed by German censors. His October 1945 lecture "Existentialism is a Humanism" made "existentialism" a fashion, and the same year he founded the review *Les Temps modernes* with Simone de Beauvoir and others. He refused the Nobel Prize in 1964.

Albert Camus (1913–1960) grew up poor in the Belcourt district of Algiers, raised by a nearly deaf, illiterate mother after his father died in the First World War. As a young journalist in Algiers he reviewed *La Nausée* in 1938. In 1942 he published *L'Étranger* and *Le Mythe de Sisyphe*; Sartre answered with a long essay, "Explication de *L'Étranger*" (1943). Camus edited the Resistance paper *Combat* and always rejected the existentialist label. The two men broke publicly in 1952 after *Les Temps modernes* attacked Camus's *L'Homme révolté*. Camus won the Nobel Prize in 1957 and died in a car crash in 1960.
