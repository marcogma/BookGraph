# Summary

All of Poe's fiction and poetry. Besides the tales covered in my other Poe notes, the stories include "The Purloined Letter" (Dupin finds a stolen letter hidden in plain sight in a letter rack), "The Gold-Bug" (a cipher leads to Captain Kidd's treasure), "The Masque of the Red Death" (a prince locks himself and his court in an abbey to escape a plague, which arrives as a masked guest), "The Pit and the Pendulum" (a prisoner of the Spanish Inquisition in Toledo is saved at the last moment as the French army enters the city), "The Premature Burial", "Ligeia", "Berenice", "MS. Found in a Bottle", "A Descent into the Maelström", the hoax "The Balloon-Hoax", and the novel *The Narrative of Arthur Gordon Pym* (1838), a sea voyage ending at the South Pole. The poems include "The Raven" (a student mourning his lost Lenore is visited by a raven that answers every question "Nevermore"), "Annabel Lee" (a dead young love in a "kingdom by the sea"), "The Bells", "Lenore", "Ulalume", "To Helen", and "A Dream Within a Dream".

# Historical & cultural context

See [[poe-and-the-invention-of-detective-fiction]]. "Annabel Lee", one of his last poems, was published two days after his death in 1849 and is usually linked to his wife Virginia, who had died two years before. "The Raven" was published in January 1845 and was reprinted and parodied widely within weeks.
