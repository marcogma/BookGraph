# What happened

In 1905 Albert Einstein, then a clerk at the Swiss patent office in Bern, published papers on the photoelectric effect (light behaves as particles), Brownian motion, and special relativity, including E = mc². His general theory of relativity (1915) described gravity as the curvature of spacetime; Arthur Eddington's measurements during the 1919 solar eclipse confirmed it and made Einstein world-famous. In the 1920s Bohr, Heisenberg, Schrödinger, and others built quantum mechanics, which Einstein helped start but never accepted as complete ("God does not play dice").

The two theories have still not been combined into one. Black holes became the main place where they meet. In 1965 Roger Penrose proved that, under general relativity, a collapsing star must form a singularity; Stephen Hawking extended the method to the universe as a whole, and in 1970 they published the Penrose–Hawking singularity theorems together. In 1974 Hawking showed that black holes should emit radiation once quantum effects are included. Hawking was diagnosed with motor neuron disease in 1963 and died in 2018. Penrose won the Nobel Prize in 2020 for his 1965 result.
