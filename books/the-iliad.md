# Summary

In the tenth year of the Greek siege of Troy, Agamemnon, leader of the Greeks, takes the captive woman Briseis from Achilles, the best Greek warrior. Achilles withdraws from the fighting and asks his mother, the goddess Thetis, to have Zeus make the Greeks lose until they honor him again. The Trojans, led by Hector, son of King Priam, push the Greeks back to their ships. Agamemnon offers gifts to bring Achilles back, and Achilles refuses. Finally Achilles lets his close companion Patroclus fight in his armor, and Hector kills Patroclus. In grief and rage Achilles reconciles with Agamemnon, gets new armor made by the god Hephaestus (the shield is described at length), and kills huge numbers of Trojans. He kills Hector in single combat and drags his body behind his chariot around Patroclus's tomb for days. The old king Priam, guided by Hermes, comes at night to Achilles' tent and kisses his hands to ransom his son's body. Achilles, thinking of his own father, weeps with him and gives the body back. The poem ends with Hector's funeral in Troy.

# Main characters

- **Achilles**: the greatest Greek warrior, whose anger is the poem's subject
- **Agamemnon**: commander of the Greek army
- **Patroclus**: Achilles' closest companion
- **Hector**: the Trojan prince and defender of the city
- **Priam**: king of Troy, Hector's father
- **Andromache**: Hector's wife
- **Helen**: the wife of Menelaus, taken by Paris, cause of the war
- **Paris**: Hector's brother
- **Menelaus**: Helen's husband, Agamemnon's brother
- **Odysseus, Ajax, Diomedes, Nestor**: other Greek leaders
- **Thetis**: Achilles' mother, a sea goddess

# Historical & cultural context

See [[homeric-epic-and-oral-tradition]]. The poem covers only about fifty days of the war. It does not include the Trojan horse or the death of Achilles, which come from other poems of the cycle and later writers.
