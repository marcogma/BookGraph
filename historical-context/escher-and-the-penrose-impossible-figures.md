# What happened

In 1954 the young mathematician Roger Penrose saw an exhibition of M. C. Escher's prints in Amsterdam during the International Congress of Mathematicians. Afterward he drew the "impossible triangle" (the tribar). With his father, the geneticist Lionel Penrose, he also designed an endless staircase that always goes up, and they published both in the *British Journal of Psychology* in 1958, sending a copy to Escher. Escher used the staircase in *Ascending and Descending* (1960) and the triangle in *Waterfall* (1961). Escher also corresponded with the geometer H. S. M. Coxeter, whose diagram of the hyperbolic plane led to the *Circle Limit* woodcuts (1958–1960).
