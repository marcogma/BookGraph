---
title: Loving the idea vs. loving the person
---

# Definition

A recurring question: when someone loves intensely and consistently despite knowing the real, flawed shape of the person they love, are they loving the actual person or an idea they built and refuse to let go of? The clearest case is the unknown woman loving a writer who barely notices her existence for over a decade — she knows exactly what he is (someone who treats women as recreations, not people) and loves him anyway, which argues for real love; but she also refuses to ever reveal herself to him, protecting the safe, platonic version over the risk of a real one, which argues for loving the idea instead. Related but distinct from [[feminine-figure-as-moral-axis]]: that concept is about a woman's structural role in a story; this one is about what "loving" someone actually consists of.

# Appears in

- [[letter-from-an-unknown-woman]] — the source text; also the Kundera comparison (lightness vs. weight as two ways of inhabiting time emotionally — one night meaning nothing to him, organizing a whole life for her)
- [[madame-bovary]] — Emma loves the *feeling* her romantic novels promised her, not any of the actual men she has affairs with; illusion is the real object of her love throughout

# Related concepts
