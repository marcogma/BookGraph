# Summary

Ten years after the fall of Troy, Odysseus still has not come home to Ithaca. Suitors occupy his house, eating his wealth and pressing his wife Penelope to remarry. She delays them by weaving a shroud by day and unweaving it at night. His son Telemachus, urged by the goddess Athena, travels to Pylos and Sparta for news of his father. Odysseus, held for seven years by the nymph Calypso, is released and shipwrecked on the island of the Phaeacians, where he tells his adventures. He and his men escaped the Lotus-eaters and the Cyclops Polyphemus, whom he blinded, which made Poseidon his enemy. They lost the bag of winds from Aeolus, fled the cannibal Laestrygonians, and stayed a year with the witch Circe, who had turned his men into pigs. He visited the underworld, sailed past the Sirens and between Scylla and Charybdis. Finally his men ate the cattle of the Sun, and all of them died in the storm that followed. The Phaeacians take him home. Disguised as a beggar, he is recognized by his old dog Argos, who then dies, and by his nurse Eurycleia from a scar. Penelope sets a contest with Odysseus's bow; only he can string it. He and Telemachus kill all the suitors. Penelope tests him with the secret of their bed, built around an olive tree. Athena stops the feud with the suitors' families.

# Main characters

- **Odysseus**: king of Ithaca, known for cunning
- **Penelope**: his faithful, clever wife
- **Telemachus**: their son
- **Athena**: the goddess who protects Odysseus
- **Poseidon**: the sea god, who persecutes him
- **Polyphemus**: the Cyclops, Poseidon's son
- **Circe**: the witch who turns his men into pigs
- **Calypso**: the nymph who holds him for seven years
- **Nausicaa**: the Phaeacian princess who finds him on the beach
- **Eumaeus**: the loyal swineherd
- **Antinous**: the worst of the suitors
