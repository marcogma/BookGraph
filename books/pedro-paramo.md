# Summary

At his dying mother's request, Juan Preciado goes to Comala to find his father, Pedro Páramo. He finds a dead town, hot and empty, where he talks with people who turn out to be ghosts, and he dies there, killed by the murmurs; he narrates from his grave, where he lies with Dorotea, a woman who spent her life begging. Through fragments out of order, the story of Pedro Páramo comes out. He takes over the Media Luna hacienda, marries Juan's mother Dolores for her land, and has his enemies killed. He fathers many children, among them the violent Miguel Páramo, who rapes women and dies falling from his horse. During the Revolution he pays off the rebels and keeps his power. He loves only Susana San Juan, a childhood friend who comes back mad, and when she dies and the town celebrates with fiesta bells, he lets Comala starve. At the end Abundio, one of his abandoned sons, drunk and grieving his wife, stabs him, and Pedro Páramo falls apart "like a pile of stones".

# Main characters

- **Juan Preciado**: the son who comes to Comala looking for his father
- **Pedro Páramo**: the cacique who owns Comala and destroys it
- **Dolores Preciado**: Juan's mother, married for her land and sent away
- **Susana San Juan**: the woman Pedro loves all his life
- **Miguel Páramo**: Pedro's favorite son, a rapist and killer
- **Father Rentería**: the priest who fails to stand up to Pedro
- **Abundio Martínez**: the mule driver, another son of Pedro's, who kills him
- **Dorotea**: the woman buried with Juan, who tells him much of the story
- **Eduviges Dyada**: the first ghost who takes Juan in

# Historical & cultural context

Published in 1955, it sold poorly at first. Rulfo worked for years as a traveling salesman for the Goodrich tire company and was also a photographer; his photographs of Jalisco are now published and exhibited.
