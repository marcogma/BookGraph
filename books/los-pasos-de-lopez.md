# Summary

A comic novel that retells the 1810 conspiracy that started Mexico's War of Independence, with the names changed. Matías Chandón, a young Spanish-born army officer, narrates how he joined a group of provincial conspirators who meet in the town of Cañada to put on plays and plot a rising against Spanish rule. Their leader is the priest Domingo Periñón (a version of Miguel Hidalgo), a lively, popular cura with a taste for theater. The plot is betrayed and has to start early and badly. The rising turns into a disorganized mass uprising with killings and looting. Periñón is captured and executed, and Chandón survives to tell it.

# Main characters

- **Matías Chandón**: the narrator, a Spanish-born officer who joins the plot
- **Domingo Periñón**: the priest who leads the rising, based on Hidalgo

# Historical & cultural context

Ibargüengoitia (1928–1983) was Mexico's best-known satirist; he also wrote *Los relámpagos de agosto* (1964), a parody of the generals of the Revolution. *Los pasos de López* (1982) mocks the official, heroic account of Hidalgo taught in Mexican schools. The historical conspiracy of Querétaro was discovered in September 1810, and Hidalgo started the revolt early with the "Grito de Dolores" on September 16; he was captured and shot in Chihuahua in 1811. Ibargüengoitia died in the 1983 Avianca plane crash near Madrid.
