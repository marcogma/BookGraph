# Summary

Momo, a ragged little girl of unknown age and origin, lives in the ruins of an old amphitheater on the edge of a big city. She has a gift: she listens so well that people who talk to her find their own answers. Her best friends are the old street-sweeper Beppo, who sweeps one stroke at a time, and the young tour guide and storyteller Gigi. Then the Men in Grey arrive: gray, cigar-smoking agents of a Timesavings Bank who persuade people to "save time" by cutting out everything not useful, such as friends, hobbies, care for the old, and play. The saved time actually goes to the Men in Grey, who live on it by smoking it. People become rushed, irritable and lonely, and children are sent to "child depots". Momo is the only one the Men in Grey can't influence. The tortoise Cassiopeia, who can see half an hour into the future, leads her to Master Secundus Minutius Hora, keeper of time, who shows her the Hour-Lilies, the flowers of everyone's time. Hora stops time, and Momo, with a single Hour-Lily, follows the Men in Grey to their vault, where they vanish one by one as their cigars run out. She frees the stolen time and it returns to the people.

# Main characters

- **Momo**: the girl who listens
- **Beppo Roadsweeper**: her old friend
- **Gigi Guide (Girolamo)**: her young friend, who becomes a rich, unhappy storyteller
- **The Men in Grey**: the time thieves
- **Master Secundus Minutius Hora**: the keeper of time
- **Cassiopeia**: Hora's tortoise, who can see thirty minutes ahead

# Historical & cultural context

Ende (1929–1995) was German and wrote *Momo* while living in Genzano, near Rome, which explains the Italian feel of the city. Published in 1973 with his own illustrations, it won the German Youth Literature Prize in 1974. Ende's father was the surrealist painter Edgar Ende. His later *The Neverending Story* (1979) was his best-known book.
