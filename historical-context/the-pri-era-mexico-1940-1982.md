# What happened

The party Calles founded in 1929 was renamed twice and became the PRI (Partido Revolucionario Institucional) in 1946. It won every presidential election until 2000. Each president served one six-year term (sexenio) and in practice chose his successor.

From 1940 to about 1970 Mexico grew fast, an era called the "Mexican miracle". Miguel Alemán (1946–1952), the first civilian president after the Revolution, pushed industrialization, Mexico City's growth, and closer ties with the United States; his government was also known for corruption. Under Gustavo Díaz Ordaz the army and paramilitaries fired on a student rally at Tlatelolco on October 2, 1968, ten days before the Olympic Games opened in Mexico City; the number of dead is still disputed. Another student march was attacked on June 10, 1971 (the Halconazo) under Luis Echeverría. The peso was devalued in 1976. José López Portillo (1976–1982) borrowed heavily against new oil discoveries, and when oil prices fell Mexico defaulted in 1982 and he nationalized the banks, weeks after promising to defend the peso "like a dog".
