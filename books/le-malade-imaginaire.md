# Summary

Argan is a healthy man convinced he is sick, spending his money on enemas and purges prescribed by his doctor, Monsieur Purgon, and his apothecary. He wants his daughter Angélique to marry Thomas Diafoirus, a ridiculous newly qualified doctor, so that he will have a doctor in the family. Angélique loves Cléante. His second wife, Béline, pretends to care for him while waiting to inherit. The maid Toinette disguises herself as a doctor to mock medicine, and Argan's brother Béralde argues that doctors know nothing. Toinette has Argan pretend to be dead to test his family: Béline celebrates, and Angélique grieves. Argan sees the truth, agrees to Angélique's marriage, and is told he can simply become a doctor himself. The play ends with a burlesque ceremony in dog Latin in which Argan is made a doctor.

# Main characters

- **Argan**: the hypochondriac
- **Béline**: his second wife, after his money
- **Angélique**: his daughter, in love with Cléante
- **Cléante**: Angélique's suitor, who sneaks in as a music teacher
- **Toinette**: the maid, who runs the plot
- **Béralde**: Argan's brother, the skeptic
- **Monsieur Purgon and the Diafoirus**: the doctors, father and son

# Historical & cultural context

See [[moliere-and-the-court-of-louis-xiv]]. This was Molière's last play (1673), a comédie-ballet with music by Marc-Antoine Charpentier, after Molière fell out with Lully. Molière, ill himself with a lung disease, played Argan and collapsed during the fourth performance, dying hours later. Béralde suggests that Argan go and see a Molière comedy about doctors, a joke Molière made at his own expense.
