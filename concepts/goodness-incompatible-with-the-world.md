---
title: Is pure goodness incompatible with the world?
---

# Definition

Raised reading *The Idiot*: Prince Myshkin doesn't self-mythologize the way the pawnbroker or the Ridiculous Man do, yet his goodness still produces catastrophe (Nastasya's death, Rogozhin's ruin). Is Dostoevsky arguing that pure goodness is simply incompatible with the world, independent of any hidden vanity? Sharpened further in discussion: maybe Myshkin's goodness is suspect not for vanity but for being too frictionless — too close to the "crystal palace" rationality that *Notes from Underground* rejects. He never says "I don't think so" to anything, which may be exactly why society calls him an idiot.
