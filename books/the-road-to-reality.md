# Summary

A book of over a thousand pages that tries to explain the physics of the universe with its full mathematics. The first half is mathematics: proof and the Pythagorean theorem, real and complex numbers, calculus, complex analysis, Riemann surfaces, Fourier analysis, hyperbolic geometry (illustrated with Escher), manifolds, vectors and tensors, Lie groups, fiber bundles, and infinity. The second half is physics: classical mechanics, Maxwell's electromagnetism, special and general relativity, quantum mechanics and quantum field theory, the Standard Model of particle physics, and Big Bang cosmology. The last part is Penrose's own view of the open problems. He argues that quantum mechanics is incomplete and that the collapse of the wave function is a real physical process involving gravity (his "objective reduction"). He is skeptical of string theory and of cosmic inflation, explains the second law of thermodynamics through the extremely low entropy of the Big Bang, and promotes his own twistor theory.

# Main characters

- **Complex numbers**: which Penrose argues underlie quantum mechanics
- **Twistor theory**: Penrose's own approach to combining geometry and quantum theory
- **Objective reduction**: his proposal that gravity causes wave-function collapse
- **Weyl curvature hypothesis**: his explanation for the low entropy of the early universe
