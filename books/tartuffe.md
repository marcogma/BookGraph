# Summary

The wealthy Parisian Orgon has taken into his house Tartuffe, a penniless man who acts extremely pious, and admires him so much that he obeys him in everything. The rest of the household, led by the outspoken maid Dorine and Orgon's brother-in-law Cléante, sees Tartuffe as a hypocrite. Orgon breaks his daughter Mariane's engagement to Valère to marry her to Tartuffe, disinherits his son Damis for accusing Tartuffe of trying to seduce his stepmother, and signs his whole property over to Tartuffe. Orgon's wife Elmire finally makes him hide under a table while Tartuffe tries to seduce her, and Orgon sees the truth. Too late: Tartuffe now owns the house and also holds a box of compromising papers Orgon was keeping for a friend in political exile. He sends a bailiff to evict the family and denounces Orgon to the king. At the last moment an officer arrives and arrests Tartuffe instead: the king has seen through him and pardons Orgon.

# Main characters

- **Orgon**: the head of the family, completely taken in
- **Tartuffe**: the religious hypocrite
- **Elmire**: Orgon's second wife, who exposes Tartuffe
- **Dorine**: the maid, who says what everyone thinks
- **Cléante**: Elmire's brother, the voice of moderation
- **Mariane and Valère**: the young lovers Orgon separates
- **Damis**: Orgon's hot-headed son
- **Madame Pernelle**: Orgon's mother, Tartuffe's other admirer

# Historical & cultural context

See [[moliere-and-the-court-of-louis-xiv]] for the five-year ban (1664–1669). The ending, where the king's officer arrives out of nowhere, is a direct compliment to Louis XIV, who had supported Molière during the ban.
