title: Process vs. outcome — Dostoevsky vs. Tolstoy
---

# Definition

Dostoevsky is a novelist of process: he shows the internal contradiction and struggle and refuses to resolve whether a transformation (the pawnbroker's grief, the Ridiculous Man's conversion) is genuine or self-mythologizing. Tolstoy is willing to grant clean, externally *witnessed* resolution — Ivan Ilyich's son kissing his hand, Vasili Andreich physically covering Nikita with his own body in the snow.

**Open question, unresolved as of this discussion:** is Tolstoy's willingness to grant witnessed transformation the more honest moral vision, or is Dostoevsky's refusal to resolve the deeper truth about human interiority?
