# Summary

In an unnamed city, a man waiting at a traffic light suddenly goes blind, seeing only white. The blindness spreads to everyone he met: the man who steals his car, the eye doctor, the doctor's patients. The government quarantines the blind in an empty mental hospital guarded by soldiers who shoot anyone who comes too close. The doctor's wife pretends to be blind to go with her husband; she is the only person who can still see. Conditions in the asylum become terrible: filth, hunger, death. A ward of blind thugs with a gun takes control of the food and demands first valuables, then women. The doctor's wife goes with the other women, and later kills the thugs' leader with scissors. A fire breaks out and the internees escape to find that the whole city has gone blind and society has collapsed. The doctor's wife leads a small group through the ruined city to her apartment: her husband, the first blind man and his wife, a girl with dark glasses, a boy with a squint, and an old man with an eye patch. A dog that licks her tears joins them. At the end the first blind man suddenly sees again, and sight returns to everyone. The doctor's wife looks at the sky, sees it white, and fears it is her turn.

# Main characters

- **The doctor's wife**: the only one who can see
- **The doctor**: an ophthalmologist
- **The first blind man and his wife**: where the epidemic starts
- **The girl with dark glasses**: a young woman who sleeps with men for money
- **The boy with the squint**: a child separated from his mother
- **The old man with the black eye patch**: who later becomes the girl's partner
- **The dog of tears**: the dog who joins them

# Historical & cultural context

See [[saramago-portugal-church-and-state]]. Published in 1995. The characters have no names and are identified by features. The sequel is *Seeing* (2004). Fernando Meirelles filmed it in 2008 with Julianne Moore as the doctor's wife.
