# Summary

A popular book on cosmology. Hawking explains how ideas of the universe developed from Aristotle and Ptolemy to Newton, then space and time in special and general relativity, and Edwin Hubble's discovery that the universe is expanding, which leads back to the Big Bang. He explains the uncertainty principle and elementary particles and forces. He describes how massive stars collapse into black holes, his and Penrose's singularity theorems, and his own discovery that black holes are not completely black but give off radiation and eventually evaporate. He discusses the origin and fate of the universe, his "no-boundary" proposal with James Hartle (that the universe has no beginning edge in imaginary time), the arrow of time, wormholes and time travel, and the search for a unified theory. He ends by saying that if we found a complete theory, we would "know the mind of God".

# Main characters

- **Event horizon**: the boundary of a black hole beyond which nothing escapes
- **Hawking radiation**: the radiation that makes black holes slowly evaporate
- **The no-boundary proposal**: Hawking and Hartle's model of the universe without a starting edge
- **Singularity**: a point where spacetime curvature becomes infinite

# Historical & cultural context

See [[twentieth-century-physics]]. Published in 1988, it stayed on the London *Sunday Times* bestseller list for 237 weeks. Hawking said his editor warned him that each equation would halve sales, so he included only E = mc². By then Hawking had lost the ability to speak after a tracheotomy in 1985 and used a speech synthesizer.
