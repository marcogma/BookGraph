# Summary

Georges Duroy, a former soldier from Normandy with good looks and no talent, is a poor railway clerk in Paris in the 1880s. An old army friend, Charles Forestier, gets him a job at the newspaper *La Vie française*. Duroy can't write, so Forestier's wife Madeleine writes his first articles. He moves up through women. He takes the married Clotilde de Marelle as a mistress (her daughter nicknames him "Bel-Ami") and marries Madeleine after Forestier dies. He seduces Virginie Walter, the wife of the paper's owner, and uses inside information about a French military expedition to Morocco that lets Walter make a fortune. He catches Madeleine with the minister Laroche-Mathieu to get a divorce and ruin the minister. Finally he runs away with Walter's young daughter Suzanne to force her father to let them marry. The book ends with the wedding at the Madeleine church, with all of Paris present, as Duroy looks toward the Chamber of Deputies and his next step.

# Main characters

- **Georges Duroy (Bel-Ami)**: the ambitious, unscrupulous hero
- **Madeleine Forestier**: the intelligent journalist who writes for him and marries him
- **Charles Forestier**: Duroy's friend, who dies of tuberculosis
- **Clotilde de Marelle**: his longest-lasting mistress
- **Monsieur Walter**: the owner of *La Vie française*, a Jewish financier
- **Virginie Walter**: Walter's wife, whom Duroy seduces and drops
- **Suzanne Walter**: their daughter, whom Duroy marries
- **Laroche-Mathieu**: the minister whose career Duroy destroys

# Historical & cultural context

See [[nineteenth-century-french-realism-and-naturalism]]. Published in 1885. Maupassant had worked as a clerk in two ministries and wrote for papers like *Gil Blas* and *Le Gaulois*, which gave him the newspaper world. The Morocco plot echoes the French invasion of Tunisia in 1881, which was followed by financial speculation.
