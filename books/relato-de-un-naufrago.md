# Summary

A first-person account of Luis Alejandro Velasco, a sailor on the Colombian navy destroyer *Caldas*. In February 1955, on the way home from repairs in Mobile, Alabama, eight crew members were washed overboard. Velasco reached a life raft and drifted for ten days in the Caribbean with no food or fresh water, fighting off sharks, catching a seagull, and trying to eat his shoes, until he reached the Colombian coast near Urabá. He became a national hero and did advertisements. The official story was that a storm caused the accident. In his account Velasco said the sea was calm, and that the men were knocked off by cargo that came loose: refrigerators, televisions, and washing machines smuggled on board by the crew.

# Main characters

- **Luis Alejandro Velasco**: the twenty-year-old sailor who survives ten days on the raft

# Historical & cultural context

See [[gabriel-garcia-marquez-aracataca-and-journalism]]. García Márquez interviewed Velasco for about twenty hours and published the story in fourteen installments in *El Espectador* in 1955, in Velasco's voice. The contraband revelation embarrassed the military government of Gustavo Rojas Pinilla, Velasco was forced out of the navy, and *El Espectador* sent García Márquez to Europe as a correspondent; the paper was closed by the government soon after. The book edition appeared in 1970 under García Márquez's name, with a preface telling this story.
