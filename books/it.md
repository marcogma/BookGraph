# Summary

In Derry, Maine, something kills children about every twenty-seven years, usually in the form of Pennywise the Dancing Clown, and the adults don't notice or forget. In 1958 seven eleven-year-old outsiders call themselves the Losers' Club: Bill Denbrough, whose little brother Georgie was killed by Pennywise in a storm drain, Ben Hanscom, Beverly Marsh, Richie Tozier, Eddie Kaspbrak, Mike Hanlon, and Stan Uris. It attacks each of them in the form of their worst fears. They are also hunted by the teenage bully Henry Bowers. Together they go into the sewers and wound It with the "Ritual of Chüd", and swear to come back if It returns. In 1985 the killings start again, and Mike, the only one who stayed in Derry, calls the others. Each has become successful and forgotten almost everything. Stan kills himself on the night of the call. The rest return, recover their memories, and face It again in its lair under the town, where It is revealed as a giant spider-like creature from outside the universe. Eddie is killed; Bill and Richie destroy It. A storm wrecks the center of Derry, and as the survivors go home their memories of Derry and of each other start fading again.

# Main characters

- **Pennywise / It**: the shape-shifting creature under Derry
- **Bill Denbrough**: the leader, a stutterer who becomes a novelist
- **Ben Hanscom**: the overweight boy who becomes an architect
- **Beverly Marsh**: the only girl in the group, abused by her father and later her husband
- **Richie Tozier**: the joker, later a radio DJ
- **Eddie Kaspbrak**: the asthmatic, controlled by his mother
- **Mike Hanlon**: the Black boy who stays in Derry as librarian
- **Stan Uris**: the orderly, rational one
- **Henry Bowers**: the violent bully
- **Georgie Denbrough**: Bill's little brother, the first victim

# Historical & cultural context

Published in 1986. King (born 1947) set much of his fiction in Maine, where he grew up and lives; Derry is loosely based on Bangor. The 1958 half draws on his own childhood in the 1950s. The novel was adapted as a TV miniseries (1990) and as two films (2017, 2019).
