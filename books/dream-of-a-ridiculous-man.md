# Notes

Nihilistic opening. The girl is the event that gives him agency for the first time in the story. In his dream he ends up corrupting the paradise he finds. My first read was that he simply realizes his actions have consequences — that he'd "closed his eyes to the beauty of life." I still think that line is the actual thesis of the story.

My second thought was less generous to him: he wants to be crucified like Jesus, feels a kind of superiority, a power to act on others. At first I didn't see that as a bad thing, since the power seemed to be about sharing love. Sitting with it more, though — going from "I ruined everything" to "I would be crucified" skips a much humbler middle step, which is just becoming an ordinary, decent person.

The ending's proportions matter too: he does find the girl again — "And I found her!... And on I go!" — but it's a half-sentence, subordinated to the larger prophetic mission he's building for himself. I think that imbalance is deliberate. Dostoevsky isn't going to resolve for me whether this is genuine humility or ego in nicer clothes.
