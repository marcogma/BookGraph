# Summary

Twenty-four case histories of patients with neurological disorders, in four parts. "Losses" opens with the title case, Dr. P., a music teacher with visual agnosia who can see details but can't recognize faces or objects; he reaches for his wife's head thinking it is his hat. In "The Lost Mariner", Jimmie G., a former sailor with Korsakoff's syndrome, can't form new memories and believes it is still 1945. In "The Disembodied Lady", Christina loses all sense of her own body (proprioception) and must watch her limbs to move them. "Excesses": conditions with too much function, such as Ray, a jazz drummer with Tourette's syndrome ("Witty Ticcy Ray") who prefers his tics on weekends. "Transports": patients with vivid reminiscences and musical seizures. "The World of the Simple": people with intellectual disabilities and special gifts, such as the twins John and Michael, who could "see" large prime numbers, and Rebecca, clumsy and slow at tests but transformed by narrative and dance.

# Main characters

- **Dr. P.**: the music teacher with visual agnosia
- **Jimmie G.**: the man stuck in 1945
- **Christina**: the woman who loses her body sense
- **Witty Ticcy Ray**: the drummer with Tourette's
- **John and Michael**: the calendar-calculating twins
- **Rebecca**: the young woman who comes alive in stories

# Historical & cultural context

Published in 1985. Sacks (1933–2015) was a British neurologist who worked in New York. His *Awakenings* (1973), about patients from the 1920s sleeping sickness epidemic briefly brought back by L-DOPA, became a film with Robin Williams. Some later critics and disability writers questioned his use of patients' stories; he changed names and details.
