# What happened

José Saramago (1922–2010) was born to a family of landless peasants in Azinhaga, Portugal. His family could not keep him in secondary school, and he trained and worked as a mechanic, then as a civil servant, translator, and journalist. He grew up under António Salazar's Estado Novo dictatorship (1933–1974) and joined the clandestine Communist Party in 1969. The Carnation Revolution of April 25, 1974, ended the dictatorship. In 1975 Saramago was deputy director of the newspaper *Diário de Notícias* and lost the job after a counter-coup that November, which pushed him to write full time. His major novels came after he was sixty.

In 1992 a Portuguese undersecretary of culture, António Sousa Lara, removed *O Evangelho segundo Jesus Cristo* from the country's shortlist for a European literary prize, saying it offended Catholics. Saramago moved to the Spanish island of Lanzarote in 1993 and lived there for the rest of his life. He won the Nobel Prize in 1998, the first writer in Portuguese to do so. His style is easy to recognize: long sentences, dialogue marked only by commas and capital letters, and few names.
