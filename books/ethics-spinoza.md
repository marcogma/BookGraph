# Notes

Spinoza describes three kinds of knowledge. Opinion is what's subject to error, where we aren't sure of anything. Belief (in the French text, *croyance*) is what we grasp through reason, but which still requires conviction in that reason — not yet certainty, just trust that the reasoning holds. Clear knowledge is what isn't only a conviction of reason but is accompanied by a feeling of joy in the thing itself — understanding that comes with its own kind of happiness attached, not just logical assent.

On love and hate: there's a love that's born from opinion — love that can be replaced the moment something better comes along, love for what's perishable, which brings suffering, and worse, love for riches, which are perishable and don't even have any real "being" of their own to be loved for. Infinite love is love of God, since God doesn't perish and leads us toward perfection. This kind of love seeks to improve every quality of one's own being, rather than seeking to possess or consume something external.
