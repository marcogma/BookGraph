# Summary

A biography of Albert Einstein from birth to death. He was born in Ulm in 1879 to a secular Jewish family, hated the rote schooling of Munich, renounced German citizenship at sixteen, and studied at the Zurich Polytechnic, where he met the Serbian physics student Mileva Marić. They had a daughter, Lieserl, before marriage, whose fate is unknown. Unable to get an academic job, he worked at the patent office in Bern and published his four 1905 papers. He developed general relativity, finished in 1915 in Berlin while his marriage collapsed; he later married his cousin Elsa. The 1919 eclipse made him world-famous. He won the 1921 Nobel Prize for the photoelectric effect. He argued with Niels Bohr for years over quantum mechanics ("God does not play dice"). As a Jew and pacifist he was targeted by the Nazis and left Germany in 1933 for the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. In 1939 he signed the letter, drafted by Leo Szilárd, warning Roosevelt that Germany might build an atomic bomb. He spent his last decades searching without success for a unified field theory, campaigned against nuclear weapons, turned down the presidency of Israel in 1952, and died in Princeton in 1955.

# Main characters

- **Albert Einstein**: the physicist
- **Mileva Marić**: his first wife, a physicist
- **Elsa Einstein**: his cousin and second wife
- **Hans Albert and Eduard**: his sons; Eduard had schizophrenia
- **Michele Besso**: his closest friend from the patent office years
- **Marcel Grossmann**: the classmate who helped him with the math of general relativity
- **Niels Bohr**: his friendly opponent on quantum mechanics
- **Arthur Eddington**: the astronomer who confirmed general relativity in 1919
