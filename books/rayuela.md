# Summary

The book can be read two ways: straight through chapters 1–56, or by a "table of instructions" that starts at chapter 73 and jumps around, mixing in "expendable chapters" of quotes, newspaper clippings, and notes by an old writer named Morelli. In "Del lado de allá" (the Paris side), Horacio Oliveira, an Argentine intellectual in his forties, wanders Paris with La Maga, a Uruguayan woman with a baby son, Rocamadour, and argues about jazz, art and metaphysics with their friends in the Club de la Serpiente. Rocamadour dies of illness in the next room during one of their talks, unnoticed for hours. La Maga disappears, possibly drowned in the Seine. Oliveira is deported. In "Del lado de acá" (the Buenos Aires side), he lives near his old friend Traveler and Traveler's wife Talita, whom he half confuses with La Maga. They work at a circus and then at a mental asylum. At the end Oliveira stands at a window above a hopscotch grid painted in the courtyard, possibly about to jump; the book does not say.

# Main characters

- **Horacio Oliveira**: the protagonist, an Argentine searching for meaning in Paris and Buenos Aires
- **La Maga (Lucía)**: his lover in Paris, intuitive where he is cerebral
- **Rocamadour**: La Maga's baby, who dies
- **Étienne, Ronald, Babs, Wong, Perico, Gregorovius**: the Club de la Serpiente, their Paris circle
- **Morelli**: an old writer whose notes on the novel appear in the expendable chapters
- **Manolo Traveler**: Oliveira's childhood friend in Buenos Aires
- **Talita**: Traveler's wife

# Historical & cultural context

See [[the-latin-american-boom]], which also covers Cortázar's move to Paris in 1951. *Rayuela* (1963) is often called a "counter-novel" because it asks the reader to choose the order. The Club de la Serpiente's jazz records and the Paris of cheap hotels and bridges come from Cortázar's own life there in the 1950s.
