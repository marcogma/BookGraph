# What happened

Jean-Baptiste Poquelin (1622–1673), son of a royal upholsterer, gave up his father's post to found a theater company in 1643 and took the stage name Molière. The company went bankrupt in Paris and toured the provinces for about thirteen years. In 1658 it played before the young Louis XIV, won his support, and settled in Paris, from 1661 at the Palais-Royal theater. Molière wrote, directed, and played the lead comic roles. With the composer Jean-Baptiste Lully he created the comédie-ballet, a play mixed with music and dance for court festivals.

His comedies drew attacks. *L'École des femmes* (1662) set off a public quarrel. *Tartuffe* was banned after its first court performance in 1664 under pressure from the Archbishop of Paris and the devout Compagnie du Saint-Sacrement, and was only allowed in public in 1669. *Dom Juan* (1665) was withdrawn after fifteen performances. On February 17, 1673, during the fourth performance of *Le Malade imaginaire*, Molière, playing the hypochondriac Argan, collapsed on stage and died that night. Actors were excluded from church rites unless they renounced their profession, and he was buried at night with little ceremony after the king intervened.

Jean de La Fontaine (1621–1695) was in the same world. Both had been protected by the finance minister Nicolas Fouquet: Molière's *Les Fâcheux* premiered at Fouquet's party at Vaux-le-Vicomte in August 1661, which La Fontaine described in a letter. Weeks later Louis XIV had Fouquet arrested. La Fontaine stayed loyal to him, which cost him royal favor for years. The first collection of his *Fables* came out in 1668.
