# Summary

A retelling of the myth of Orestes. Years after Clytemnestra and her lover Aegisthus murdered King Agamemnon, Orestes, raised abroad, returns incognito to Argos. The city is covered in flies and its people live in ritual guilt and repentance for the murder, as Aegisthus and the god Jupiter want, because guilty people are easy to rule. His sister Electra is treated as a servant. Jupiter tries to make Orestes leave. Instead Orestes decides to act: he kills Aegisthus and Clytemnestra and claims the act as his own, refusing all remorse. He tells Jupiter that once a man knows he is free, the gods have no more power over him. Electra, terrified, repents and submits to Jupiter. Orestes leaves the city, drawing the flies (the Furies) after him like the Pied Piper.

# Main characters

- **Orestes**: the son who chooses to act and accepts his freedom
- **Electra**: his sister, who wants revenge and then can't bear it
- **Jupiter**: the god who rules through guilt
- **Aegisthus**: the king, who rules through the same fear
- **Clytemnestra**: Orestes' mother, Agamemnon's murderer

# Historical & cultural context

See [[french-existentialism]]. First performed in June 1943 in occupied Paris, directed by Charles Dullin at the Théâtre de la Cité (the renamed Théâtre Sarah-Bernhardt, renamed by the Germans because Bernhardt was Jewish). The German censors let it through. Sartre later said the play was about refusing the Vichy government's message that the French should repent for their defeat. The myth's best-known ancient version is Aeschylus's *Oresteia*.
