# Summary

A parable about Pahom, a peasant who believes that if only he had enough land, he'd fear no one, not even the devil. As he acquires more and more through a series of deals, his ambition grows with it, culminating in an offer from the Bashkirs: he can have all the land he can walk around in a single day for a fixed price, as long as he's back at his starting point by sunset. Pahom walks farther than he should, and in his desperate final sprint to the mark, he collapses and dies just as he reaches it — his servant then buries him in a plot exactly the length of a man, answering the title literally.

# Main characters

- **Pahom** — the peasant protagonist, whose growing ambition for land destroys him
- **The Bashkirs** — the nomadic people who offer him the fatal land deal
- **The Devil** — appears briefly near the start, watching and tempting Pahom's growing greed

# Notes

Reads more like a didactic fable than either Dostoevsky's psychological confession or Tolstoy's own witnessed-redemption stories like *Ivan Ilyich* — the punishment fits the sin with almost mathematical precision. Haven't dug into this one deeply yet; want to give it its own real pass rather than folding it into the same frame as the others.
