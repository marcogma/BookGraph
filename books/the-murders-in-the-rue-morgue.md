# Summary

In Paris, the narrator lives with C. Auguste Dupin, a poor aristocrat who loves analysis and can follow other people's trains of thought. The newspapers report the savage murder of Madame L'Espanaye and her daughter in a locked fourth-floor room on the Rue Morgue: the daughter strangled and stuffed up the chimney, the mother nearly decapitated in the yard. Witnesses heard two voices, one French and one shrill voice that each witness took for a different foreign language. Dupin examines the room, finds that a window that seemed nailed shut has a broken nail and a spring, and notes the inhuman strength and agility the crime required. He concludes the killer was an orangutan. He places an ad in the paper saying an orangutan has been found, and a sailor comes to claim it. The sailor admits that the animal escaped from him with his razor, climbed into the apartment, and killed the women while he watched from the lightning rod.

# Main characters

- **C. Auguste Dupin**: the analytical detective
- **The narrator**: Dupin's unnamed friend
- **The sailor**: the owner of the orangutan
- **Prefect G.**: the head of the Paris police, outdone by Dupin

# Historical & cultural context

See [[poe-and-the-invention-of-detective-fiction]]: published in *Graham's Magazine* in 1841, this is considered the first modern detective story. Dupin returns in "The Mystery of Marie Rogêt" (1842) and "The Purloined Letter" (1844).
