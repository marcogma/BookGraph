# What happened

Donatien Alphonse François, Marquis de Sade (1740–1814), was an aristocrat and army officer whose sexual abuse scandals (the Rose Keller affair of 1768, the Marseille affair of 1772) led his mother-in-law to obtain a royal *lettre de cachet* against him. He was held at Vincennes from 1777 and moved to the Bastille in 1784. There, in 1785, he wrote *Les 120 Journées de Sodome* on a roll of paper about twelve meters long, hidden in his cell. On July 4, 1789, after shouting to the crowd from his window that prisoners were being killed, he was moved to the asylum at Charenton. The Bastille was stormed ten days later and he lost the manuscript, which he said he mourned with "tears of blood".

Freed in 1790, he took part in revolutionary politics in his Paris section, was jailed again under the Terror, and narrowly escaped the guillotine. He published *Justine* anonymously in 1791. In 1801, under Napoleon, he was arrested as the author of *Justine* and *Juliette* and held without trial, finally at Charenton, where he died in 1814. The scroll of *Les 120 Journées* resurfaced and was first published in 1904 by the German psychiatrist Iwan Bloch. France declared it a national treasure in 2017 and bought it in 2021.
