# Summary

A collection of Escher's prints (woodcuts, wood engravings and lithographs) with his own introduction and short explanations of the works. The book follows his development: early landscapes of southern Italy, then the tessellations he developed after visiting the Alhambra in Granada, where interlocking birds, fish and reptiles fill a plane (*Day and Night*, 1938; *Metamorphosis*, *Reptiles*, 1943), and the "impossible" architecture and infinity prints: *Drawing Hands* (1948), *Relativity* (1953), with stairs going in three directions of gravity, *Print Gallery* (1956), *Circle Limit* (1958–60), *Ascending and Descending* (1960), with monks on an endless staircase, and *Waterfall* (1961), where water flows down and returns to its source.

# Main characters

- **Tessellation**: covering a plane with interlocking shapes, without gaps
- **Impossible figures**: objects that can be drawn but not built
- **Metamorphosis**: shapes that gradually change into other shapes across a print

# Historical & cultural context

See [[escher-and-the-penrose-impossible-figures]]. Escher (1898–1972) was Dutch and had no mathematical training; mathematicians discovered his work in the 1950s and he corresponded with several of them. He visited the Alhambra in 1922 and again in 1936, making careful copies of the Moorish tile patterns, which led to his tessellations.
