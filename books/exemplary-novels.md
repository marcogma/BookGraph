# Summary

Twelve short novels published in 1613. Some are idealized romances of lost children, kidnappings and recognitions: "La gitanilla" (a girl raised by gypsies turns out to be noble, and a nobleman lives as a gypsy to win her), "La española inglesa", "La fuerza de la sangre" (a woman raped in the dark later finds and marries the man), "La ilustre fregona", "Las dos doncellas", "La señora Cornelia", "El amante liberal". Others are realistic and satirical: "Rinconete y Cortadillo" (two boys join a thieves' guild in Seville run like a religious brotherhood), "El licenciado Vidriera" (a scholar believes he is made of glass and becomes a sharp critic of society), "El celoso extremeño" (an old man locks up his young wife and is fooled anyway). The last two are linked: in "El casamiento engañoso" a soldier tricked into marriage tells of overhearing, in the hospital, two dogs talking, and "El coloquio de los perros" is their dialogue, a picaresque life told by the dog Berganza.

# Historical & cultural context

See [[the-spanish-golden-age-and-the-picaresque]]. Cervantes says in the prologue that he was the first to write novellas in Spanish rather than translating Italian ones. "Exemplary" refers to the moral lesson he claims each one carries. The book came out between the two parts of *Don Quixote*.
