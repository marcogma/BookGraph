# What happened

London's first permanent public playhouses were built from 1576 (The Theatre, then the Curtain, the Rose, and in 1599 the Globe), outside the City's jurisdiction because the City authorities opposed plays. Companies competed for audiences that mixed apprentices and nobles. The Admiral's Men, with the star actor Edward Alleyn, played Marlowe; the Lord Chamberlain's Men, with Richard Burbage and Shakespeare as a sharer, played Shakespeare.

Christopher Marlowe and William Shakespeare were both born in 1564. Marlowe, a Cambridge scholar who may have worked as a government spy, made blank verse (unrhymed iambic pentameter) the standard for English drama, and Shakespeare's early plays show his influence. In May 1593, under investigation for atheism, Marlowe was stabbed to death in a house in Deptford by Ingram Frizer, in the company of men with intelligence connections. Shakespeare kept writing for another twenty years; *Hamlet* dates from about 1599–1601.
