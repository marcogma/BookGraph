# Summary

Arnolphe, a middle-aged man terrified of being made a cuckold, has had the girl Agnès raised in a convent in total ignorance so that she will be a docile, faithful wife. He has also taken a noble-sounding name, Monsieur de la Souche. While he is away, a young man, Horace, son of his friend, meets Agnès and falls in love with her. Horace does not know that Arnolphe and "M. de la Souche" are the same person, so he keeps telling Arnolphe every step of his plan to win her, and every one of Arnolphe's countermeasures fails. Agnès, with no education in deceit, simply says what she feels and outwits him. At the end it turns out that Agnès is the long-lost daughter of Enrique, who has arranged for her to marry Horace, the son of his friend Oronte. Arnolphe leaves with a cry of despair.

# Main characters

- **Arnolphe (M. de la Souche)**: the jealous guardian who wants a wife kept in ignorance
- **Agnès**: his ward, naive but not stupid
- **Horace**: the young man who loves her and confides in her guardian
- **Chrysalde**: Arnolphe's friend, who tells him his system won't work
- **Alain and Georgette**: Arnolphe's comic servants
- **Enrique and Oronte**: the fathers whose return resolves the plot

# Historical & cultural context

See [[moliere-and-the-court-of-louis-xiv]]. The 1662 play was Molière's biggest success to that point and set off a public quarrel: rivals called it indecent and irreligious (Arnolphe's "Maxims of Marriage" parody religious instruction). Molière replied with two short plays, *La Critique de l'École des femmes* and *L'Impromptu de Versailles* (1663). That year he had married Armande Béjart, about twenty years younger than him, and his enemies joked about the parallel.
