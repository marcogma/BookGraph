# Summary

Seven generations of the Buendía family in Macondo, a town founded in a swamp by José Arcadio Buendía and his cousin-wife Úrsula Iguarán, who fear their child will be born with a pig's tail because they are related. The gypsy Melquíades brings ice, magnets, and parchments nobody can read. The town grows, gets a government, and is pulled into decades of civil war; Colonel Aureliano Buendía leads thirty-two uprisings and loses them all. The family repeats the same names (José Arcadio, Aureliano) and the same passions, including near-incest. A foreign banana company arrives and turns Macondo into a plantation town; when workers strike, the army kills about three thousand of them at the station and the bodies are carried off by train, and afterward everyone denies it happened. A rain of four years, eleven months and two days follows, and the town declines. At the end Aureliano Babilonia has a child with his aunt Amaranta Úrsula; she dies in childbirth, the baby is born with a pig's tail and is eaten by ants. Aureliano finally deciphers Melquíades' parchments, which tell the family's whole history in advance, and as he reads the last line a wind wipes Macondo off the earth.

# Main characters

- **José Arcadio Buendía**: founder of Macondo, obsessed with science and alchemy, ends his life tied to a chestnut tree
- **Úrsula Iguarán**: his wife, who holds the family together for more than a century
- **Colonel Aureliano Buendía**: their son, the Liberal rebel who later makes little gold fishes and melts them down
- **José Arcadio**: their oldest son, huge and impulsive, who marries his adopted sister Rebeca
- **Amaranta**: their daughter, who refuses every suitor and weaves her own burial shroud
- **Rebeca**: the adopted girl who eats earth
- **Melquíades**: the gypsy whose parchments contain the family's history
- **Pilar Ternera**: fortune-teller and lover of two Buendía brothers
- **Remedios the Beauty**: so beautiful that men die for her; she rises to heaven while folding sheets
- **Fernanda del Carpio**: a rigid highland aristocrat who marries into the family
- **Aureliano Babilonia**: the last Buendía to read the parchments
- **Amaranta Úrsula**: his aunt and lover, mother of the child with the pig's tail

# Historical & cultural context

See [[gabriel-garcia-marquez-aracataca-and-journalism]] for the Aracataca, grandparents, and banana-massacre background, and [[the-latin-american-boom]] for how the book's 1967 publication turned the Boom into a mass phenomenon. The book is the best-known example of what critics call "magical realism", a term García Márquez himself said described the ordinary reality of the Caribbean coast. The first edition, from Editorial Sudamericana in Buenos Aires, sold out within weeks.
