# Summary

A retelling of the Old Testament through Cain. After Adam and Eve are thrown out of the garden, Cain kills his brother Abel in a jealous rage, but he blames God, who rejected his offering for no reason and could have stopped the killing. God marks his forehead and condemns him to wander. Riding a donkey, Cain finds himself moving between different moments of biblical time. He lives as the lover of Lilith in the city of Nod. He stops Abraham's hand before he can kill Isaac. He sees the Tower of Babel fall, Sodom and Gomorrah destroyed with their children, the Israelites killed for worshipping the golden calf, the fall of Jericho, and Job's suffering under God's bet with Satan. Each time he concludes that God is cruel and jealous. Finally he boards Noah's ark and kills Noah's family one by one, and Noah throws himself into the sea, so that humanity ends. When God arrives, Cain tells him what he has done, and the two argue, as the narrator says they still are.

# Main characters

- **Cain**: the first murderer, who becomes God's accuser
- **God**: the Lord, portrayed as jealous and violent
- **Lilith**: the woman Cain lives with in Nod
- **Abraham and Isaac**: whose sacrifice Cain stops
- **Noah**: whose ark Cain sabotages

# Historical & cultural context

See [[saramago-portugal-church-and-state]]. Published in 2009, the year before Saramago's death, and his last completed novel. The Portuguese Catholic Church criticized it, and a Portuguese member of the European Parliament suggested Saramago give up his citizenship; Saramago answered that he was not afraid. It continues the argument of his 1991 *O Evangelho segundo Jesus Cristo*.
