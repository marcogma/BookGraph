# Summary

At thirty-seven, Toru Watanabe hears the Beatles' "Norwegian Wood" on a plane landing in Hamburg and remembers his student years in Tokyo in the late 1960s. In high school his best friend Kizuki killed himself at seventeen, with no explanation. In Tokyo, Toru meets Kizuki's girlfriend Naoko again; they walk around the city every Sunday, and on her twentieth birthday they sleep together. She then disappears into a sanatorium in the mountains near Kyoto, where Toru visits her and meets her roommate Reiko, an older woman and former music teacher who plays guitar. At university, during the student strikes, he meets Midori, a lively, outspoken classmate who is caring for her dying father. He spends nights out with his wealthy, cynical dorm-mate Nagasawa, whose devoted girlfriend Hatsumi later kills herself. Toru is caught between Naoko and Midori. Naoko hangs herself in the woods. Toru wanders the coast for a month in grief. Reiko comes to Tokyo, plays Naoko's favorite songs with him at a "funeral", and sleeps with him before leaving for a new life. Toru phones Midori and tells her he wants to start again with her. She asks, "Where are you now?", and he doesn't know where he is.

# Main characters

# Historical & cultural context

Published in 1987, it sold millions of copies in Japan and made Murakami (born 1949) a celebrity, which he found uncomfortable; he lived abroad for several years afterward. It is his most realistic novel, without the surreal elements of most of his work. The late-1960s student movement at Tokyo's universities, which Murakami lived through as a student at Waseda, is the background. The title comes from the Beatles song (1965).
