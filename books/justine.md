# Summary

Two orphaned sisters are left with nothing at ages twelve and fifteen. Juliette chooses vice and becomes rich; Justine chooses virtue and suffers. Years later, under another name, Justine tells her story at an inn to a rich lady. Each time she tries to do good, she is punished. She is jailed for a theft she refused to commit and exploited by a band of thieves led by La Dubois. The young aristocrat Bressac betrays her after she tries to stop his plan to poison his mother. The surgeon Rodin brands her, and libertine monks hold her captive in their monastery. Every libertine she meets explains, at length, a philosophy of nature in which the strong are right to use the weak and virtue is foolish. The lady turns out to be Juliette, now Madame de Lorsange. She takes Justine in, and Justine is killed by a bolt of lightning.

# Main characters

- **Justine (Sophie)**: the virtuous sister
- **Juliette (Madame de Lorsange)**: the vicious sister
- **La Dubois**: leader of the thieves
- **Bressac**: the young noble who wants to poison his mother
- **Rodin**: the surgeon
- **Dom Sévérino**: head of the monastery of Sainte-Marie-des-Bois

# Historical & cultural context

See [[sade-imprisonment-and-the-french-revolution]]. Sade wrote a first version, *Les Infortunes de la vertu*, in the Bastille in 1787. The 1791 *Justine* is the expanded version; a third, *La Nouvelle Justine* (1797), came with *Juliette*. The book is a parody of the 18th-century sentimental novel, such as Samuel Richardson's *Pamela*, in which virtue is rewarded.
