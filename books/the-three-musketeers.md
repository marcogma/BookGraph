# Summary

In 1625 the young Gascon d'Artagnan comes to Paris to join the king's musketeers. On his first day he challenges three musketeers, Athos, Porthos and Aramis, to duels, and when the Cardinal's guards interrupt, he fights alongside them and becomes their friend. Their enemy is Cardinal Richelieu, Louis XIII's chief minister, and his agents, the Comte de Rochefort and the beautiful spy Milady de Winter. To save Queen Anne of Austria from scandal, d'Artagnan travels to London to recover the diamond studs she gave her lover, the Duke of Buckingham, before the king notices. During the siege of the Protestant city of La Rochelle, Milady arranges Buckingham's assassination by the Puritan Felton and poisons Constance Bonacieux, d'Artagnan's love. Athos reveals Milady was his wife, branded as a criminal, whom he had tried to hang. The four friends and others try her and have her beheaded by the executioner of Lille. Richelieu, impressed by d'Artagnan, makes him a lieutenant of the musketeers.

# Main characters

- **D'Artagnan**: the young Gascon hero
- **Athos**: the oldest musketeer, a gloomy nobleman
- **Porthos**: the vain, strong musketeer
- **Aramis**: the musketeer who wants to be a priest
- **Milady de Winter**: Richelieu's spy, the villain
- **Cardinal Richelieu**: Louis XIII's chief minister
- **The Comte de Rochefort**: Richelieu's agent
- **Constance Bonacieux**: d'Artagnan's love, the queen's seamstress
- **Queen Anne of Austria**: the queen d'Artagnan protects
- **The Duke of Buckingham**: the English minister in love with the queen
- **Planchet**: d'Artagnan's servant

# Historical & cultural context

Dumas (1802–1870) wrote the novel with his collaborator Auguste Maquet and serialized it in *Le Siècle* in 1844. It is loosely based on *Mémoires de M. d'Artagnan* (1700) by Gatien de Courtilz de Sandras. The historical Charles de Batz de Castelmore d'Artagnan (about 1611–1673) was a musketeer captain who arrested Fouquet in 1661 and died at the siege of Maastricht. The siege of La Rochelle (1627–1628) and Buckingham's assassination by John Felton (1628) are real. Dumas's father, General Thomas-Alexandre Dumas, was born in Saint-Domingue to a French nobleman and an enslaved African woman and became a general of the French Revolution.
