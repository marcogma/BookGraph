# Summary

The young Perdican returns to his father the Baron's château after his studies, at the same time as his cousin Camille, who has spent years in a convent. The Baron wants them to marry. Camille, taught by the nuns that men betray women, coldly refuses Perdican. Hurt, he courts Rosette, a peasant girl who is his foster sister, partly to make Camille jealous, and stages a scene where Camille can see him offering Rosette a ring. Both cousins play games of pride and both are in love. When they finally admit their love to each other in the chapel, Rosette, hidden behind the altar, overhears, and dies of shock. Camille says "Elle est morte. Adieu, Perdican." The comedy of the two tutors and the Baron turns to tragedy in the last scene.

# Main characters

- **Perdican**: the young graduate who returns home
- **Camille**: his cousin, raised in a convent
- **Rosette**: the peasant girl Perdican uses to provoke Camille
- **The Baron**: Perdican's father, who wants the marriage
- **Maître Blazius and Maître Bridaine**: two greedy, drunken clerics
- **Dame Pluche**: Camille's governess

# Historical & cultural context

Musset (1810–1857) was one of the main French Romantic writers. He wrote the play in 1834, after his stormy affair with the novelist George Sand, which ended in Venice that year when she left him for his doctor; some of Camille's lines come from Sand's letters. After a failed premiere in 1830 Musset wrote plays to be read rather than staged ("Un spectacle dans un fauteuil"), and this one was only performed in 1861, after his death.
