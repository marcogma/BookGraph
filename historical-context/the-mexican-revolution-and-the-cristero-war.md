# What happened

The Mexican Revolution began in 1910 with Francisco Madero's revolt against Porfirio Díaz, who had ruled since 1876. It turned into a decade of civil war among factions led by Madero, Victoriano Huerta, Venustiano Carranza, Pancho Villa, Emiliano Zapata, and Álvaro Obregón. Around a million people died or emigrated out of a population of about fifteen million. The 1917 Constitution promised land reform (ejidos) and sharply restricted the Catholic Church.

When President Plutarco Elías Calles enforced those religious articles in 1926, armed Catholic peasants rose up in western Mexico, mostly in Jalisco, Michoacán, Colima, and Zacatecas, under the cry "¡Viva Cristo Rey!". The Cristero War lasted until a 1929 settlement brokered with US help and killed about 90,000 combatants. Land reform was slow and uneven, and in much of rural Jalisco the old haciendas and local bosses (caciques) kept power well into the 1930s. Calles founded the ruling party, the PNR, in 1929.

Juan Rulfo (1917–1986) grew up in this world, in southern Jalisco. His father was murdered in 1923 and his mother died in 1927, during the Cristero War, and he spent part of his childhood in an orphanage in Guadalajara. He published only two books.
