# Summary

A novel in dialogue. The young nobleman Calisto, chasing his falcon into a garden, meets Melibea and falls in love; she rejects him. His servant Sempronio suggests hiring Celestina, an old go-between, former prostitute, brothel keeper and witch. Celestina works on Melibea with a spell and with her talk, and Melibea gives in. Calisto pays Celestina with a gold chain. His servants Sempronio and Pármeno, whom Celestina has bought with the prostitutes Elicia and Areúsa, demand their share, and when she refuses they kill her. They are caught and executed in the square. Calisto and Melibea meet in her garden at night. Areúsa and Elicia arrange revenge through the bully Centurio. One night Calisto, rushing down the garden ladder at a noise, falls and dies. Melibea climbs a tower, tells her father Pleberio everything, and jumps. The book ends with Pleberio's lament over her body against love, fortune and the world.

# Main characters

- **Calisto**: the young nobleman in love
- **Melibea**: the daughter of a rich family
- **Celestina**: the old go-between, the book's real center
- **Sempronio and Pármeno**: Calisto's servants, who kill Celestina
- **Elicia and Areúsa**: the prostitutes in Celestina's circle
- **Pleberio**: Melibea's father
- **Lucrecia**: Melibea's maid

# Historical & cultural context

See [[the-spanish-golden-age-and-the-picaresque]]. The first known edition (Burgos, 1499) has sixteen acts. In 1502 it was expanded to twenty-one, and the title changed from comedy to *Tragicomedia*. In an acrostic poem in the prologue, Rojas says he found the first act already written and finished the rest in fifteen days of vacation as a law student at Salamanca. He was a *converso* from a family of converted Jews, and later a lawyer and mayor of Talavera.
