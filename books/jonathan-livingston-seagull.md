# Summary

Jonathan Livingston Seagull is not interested in fighting for scraps of food like the rest of his flock; he wants to learn to fly faster and better. He experiments with dives and speed, crashes, and keeps trying. The flock, which believes gulls exist only to eat, expels him for irresponsibility. He lives alone and keeps perfecting his flight. Two shining gulls take him to a higher plane, where he learns from the elder Chiang that perfect speed is "being there" and that he can fly anywhere by thought. He returns to his old flock to teach outcast gulls, first Fletcher Lynd Seagull, who become his students. When Fletcher is nearly killed in a crash and recovers, the flock calls Jonathan a devil. Jonathan leaves, telling Fletcher to keep teaching and to see the good in every gull. In the fourth part added in 2014, after Jonathan's departure the flock turns him into an object of worship and ritual, forgetting the flying, until a young gull rediscovers the joy of flight.

# Main characters

# Historical & cultural context

Bach (born 1936) was a former US Air Force pilot and wrote about flying for magazines. The book, illustrated with Russell Munson's photographs of gulls, was rejected by many publishers and came out in 1970, then topped the *New York Times* bestseller list for over a year. It was read in the 1970s as a spiritual fable, and Hall Bartlett made a film of it in 1973 with music by Neil Diamond.
