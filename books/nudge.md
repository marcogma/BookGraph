# Summary

Thaler and Sunstein distinguish "Econs", the perfectly rational people of economic theory, from "Humans", who are biased, distracted, and lazy. Since every choice is presented somehow, someone is always a "choice architect": the way a cafeteria arranges food, or which option a form ticks by default, changes what people choose. They propose "libertarian paternalism": design choices to steer people toward what they would want for themselves (a "nudge"), while leaving them free to choose otherwise. Examples include automatic enrollment in retirement savings, Thaler's "Save More Tomorrow" plan where workers commit future raises to savings, default organ donation, simplified mortgage and credit card disclosures, the fly painted in the urinals at Amsterdam's Schiphol airport, and reminders that most people pay their taxes. They also address objections about manipulation.

# Main characters

- **Choice architecture**: the way options are presented
- **Default**: the option that happens if you do nothing
- **Libertarian paternalism**: steering choices while keeping freedom
- **Econs and Humans**: theoretical rational actors versus real people
- **Save More Tomorrow**: Thaler and Shlomo Benartzi's savings plan
