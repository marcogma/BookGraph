# What happened

The *Iliad* and *Odyssey* are set around the Trojan War, which, if it happened, would date to the Late Bronze Age, around 1200 BCE. The poems were composed much later, probably in the 8th or 7th century BCE, in dactylic hexameter, and were first written down after the Greeks adopted the alphabet. Who "Homer" was, and whether one poet composed both, is the old "Homeric Question". In the 1930s the American scholar Milman Parry and his student Albert Lord recorded illiterate singers in Yugoslavia who composed long epics live using stock phrases. They argued the Homeric poems came out of a similar oral tradition, which explains repeated epithets like "swift-footed Achilles" and "rosy-fingered Dawn". In the 1870s Heinrich Schliemann excavated Hisarlik in Turkey, which most archaeologists now accept as the site of Troy.

Older still is the Mesopotamian *Epic of Gilgamesh*, whose standard version dates to about 1200 BCE. The classicist M. L. West, in *The East Face of Helicon* (1997), argued that Greek epic borrowed from Near Eastern poetry, citing for example the parallel between Gilgamesh mourning Enkidu and Achilles mourning Patroclus.
