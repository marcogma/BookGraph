# Summary

Kahneman describes the mind as two systems: System 1, fast, automatic, and intuitive, and System 2, slow, effortful, and lazy, which usually accepts what System 1 suggests. Part 1 shows how System 1 jumps to conclusions, with "what you see is all there is". Part 2 covers heuristics and biases: anchoring, the availability heuristic, the representativeness heuristic and the "Linda problem", base-rate neglect, and regression to the mean. Part 3 is about overconfidence: the illusion of understanding, the illusion of skill among stock pickers, the weakness of expert intuition outside regular environments, and the planning fallacy. Part 4 explains prospect theory: people judge outcomes as gains and losses relative to a reference point, losses hurt about twice as much as gains please, and framing changes choices. Part 5 separates the "experiencing self" from the "remembering self": memory judges an experience by its peak and its end (the colonoscopy study) and ignores duration.

# Main characters

- **System 1 and System 2**: fast intuitive thinking and slow deliberate thinking
- **WYSIATI**: "what you see is all there is": judging from available information only
- **Anchoring**: being pulled toward an initial number
- **Availability**: judging frequency by how easily examples come to mind
- **Loss aversion**: losses weigh more than equal gains
- **Peak-end rule**: memories judged by the worst moment and the end
- **Amos Tversky**: Kahneman's longtime collaborator
