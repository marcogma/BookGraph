# Summary

In the last years of Louis XIV, four rich libertines (a duke, his brother the bishop, a judge, and a banker) lock themselves for the winter in the remote Château de Silling in the Black Forest. With them are their wives, kidnapped adolescents, servants, and four aging prostitutes who serve as storytellers. Each month one storyteller recounts 150 "passions", ordered from simple to criminal to murderous, and the libertines act them out on the captives. Only the first part (November, the simple passions) is written as full narrative. The other three months exist as Sade's plan in note form, listing the passions and, at the end, which victims were killed and which of the group returned to Paris.

# Main characters

- **The Duc de Blangis**: the most powerful of the four libertines
- **The Bishop**: his brother
- **The Président de Curval**: a judge
- **Durcet**: a financier
- **Duclos, Champville, Martaine, Desgranges**: the four storytellers

# Historical & cultural context

See [[sade-imprisonment-and-the-french-revolution]] for how the book was written on a scroll in the Bastille, lost in 1789, and published in 1904. Pier Paolo Pasolini adapted it as *Salò, or the 120 Days of Sodom* (1975), moving it to Mussolini's last fascist republic; Pasolini was murdered weeks before the film's release.
