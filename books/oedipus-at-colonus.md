# Summary

Years after his fall, the old, blind Oedipus has been wandering as a beggar, guided by his daughter Antigone. They arrive at Colonus, near Athens, and sit in a grove sacred to the Eumenides (the Furies), where an oracle said Oedipus would die. The locals want to expel him, but Theseus, king of Athens, grants him protection. His other daughter Ismene brings news that his sons are fighting over Thebes, and that an oracle says whoever holds Oedipus's grave will be protected. Creon arrives from Thebes to take him back by force and seizes the daughters; Theseus rescues them. Oedipus's son Polynices, about to attack Thebes, asks for his blessing, and Oedipus curses both sons to die by each other's hands. Thunder signals his end. Oedipus, no longer needing a guide, leads them to the place of his death. Only Theseus sees how he dies, and the location of the grave is to stay secret to protect Athens.

# Main characters

- **Oedipus**: old, blind, and insisting he did not sin knowingly
- **Antigone**: his daughter and guide
- **Ismene**: his other daughter
- **Theseus**: king of Athens, who protects him
- **Creon**: now ruling Thebes, who tries to take Oedipus back
- **Polynices**: Oedipus's elder son

# Historical & cultural context

See [[fifth-century-athens]]. Sophocles' last play, written near the end of his life, when he was about ninety and Athens was losing the Peloponnesian War. Colonus was his own birthplace. His grandson, also named Sophocles, staged it after his death, in 401 BCE. The three "Theban plays" were not written as a trilogy: *Antigone* came first, about forty years before this one.
