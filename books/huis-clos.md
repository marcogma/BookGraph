# Summary

Three dead people are brought by a valet into a room in hell decorated with Second Empire furniture: no mirrors, no windows, lights that never go off, and no instruments of torture. Garcin, a journalist, says he was shot for being a pacifist. Inès, a postal clerk, is a lesbian who took her cousin's wife Florence away from him; the cousin died under a tram and Florence gassed herself and Inès. Estelle, a rich young woman, says she does not know why she is there. They slowly force each other's confessions: Garcin was shot while trying to flee the war and treated his wife cruelly; Estelle drowned the baby she had with her lover, who then shot himself. Inès wants Estelle, Estelle wants Garcin, and Garcin wants Inès to believe he is not a coward. When the door suddenly opens, nobody leaves. Garcin realizes there is no need for torturers: "L'enfer, c'est les autres". Estelle tries to stab Inès with a paper knife, which is useless since they are dead, and they laugh. The play ends with Garcin saying "Eh bien, continuons."

# Main characters

- **Garcin**: a journalist, a deserter who needs to be seen as brave
- **Inès**: a sharp postal clerk who caused two deaths
- **Estelle**: a rich young woman who killed her baby
- **The valet**: who brings them to the room

# Historical & cultural context

See [[french-existentialism]]. Written in late 1943 and first performed in May 1944 at the Théâtre du Vieux-Colombier in occupied Paris, a few weeks before D-Day. Sartre wrote it for three friends so they would have equal parts, and kept it to one act with no intermission.
