# Summary

Harpagon, a rich widower, is obsessed with his money and has buried a box of ten thousand gold écus in his garden. He plans to marry his daughter Élise to the old, rich Anselme because Anselme will take her without a dowry ("sans dot!"), and he plans to marry the young, poor Mariane himself. His son Cléante is in love with Mariane, and Élise is in love with Valère, who has taken a job as Harpagon's steward to be near her. Cléante, in debt, tries to borrow money through a broker and discovers the usurer is his own father. Cléante's servant La Flèche steals the money box, and Harpagon delivers a frantic monologue about his stolen money. Everything is resolved when Anselme turns out to be the father of both Valère and Mariane, lost in a shipwreck years before. The young couples marry, and Harpagon happily gets his box back on condition that Anselme pays for both weddings.

# Main characters

- **Harpagon**: the miser
- **Cléante**: his son, in love with Mariane
- **Élise**: his daughter, in love with Valère
- **Valère**: Élise's lover, working as steward
- **Mariane**: the poor young woman both father and son want
- **La Flèche**: Cléante's servant, who steals the box
- **Frosine**: a go-between
- **Maître Jacques**: Harpagon's cook and coachman
- **Anselme**: the rich old man who turns out to be Valère and Mariane's father

# Historical & cultural context

See [[moliere-and-the-court-of-louis-xiv]]. Molière adapted it (1668) from *Aulularia* by the Roman playwright Plautus, in which a poor man finds a pot of gold. It was written in prose, which was unusual for a five-act comedy at the time; Molière played Harpagon.
