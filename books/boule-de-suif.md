# Summary

The edition I logged has "Boule de suif" plus twenty other stories; this summary covers the title story. In the winter of 1870–71, during the Franco-Prussian War, a coach leaves occupied Rouen for Le Havre with ten passengers. They are three bourgeois couples (wine merchants, a factory owner, and a count), two nuns, the democrat Cornudet, and a plump prostitute nicknamed Boule de Suif ("ball of fat"). Stuck without food, the respectable passengers look down on her, then gladly eat the food she shares. At an inn in Tôtes, a Prussian officer refuses to let the coach leave until she sleeps with him. She refuses out of patriotism. Over several days the others, including the nuns, pressure her with stories of heroic sacrifice until she gives in. The next morning they shun her again, and eat their own food in front of her without offering her any. She cries while Cornudet whistles "La Marseillaise".

# Main characters

- **Boule de Suif (Élisabeth Rousset)**: the prostitute, more patriotic than the respectable passengers
- **Loiseau and his wife**: wine merchants
- **Carré-Lamadon and his wife**: a factory owner and his wife
- **The Comte and Comtesse de Bréville**: the aristocrats
- **Cornudet**: a republican who talks big and does nothing
- **The Prussian officer**: who holds the coach until she gives in

# Historical & cultural context

See [[nineteenth-century-french-realism-and-naturalism]]: "Boule de suif" was Maupassant's contribution to *Les Soirées de Médan* (1880), the story that made his name, praised by Flaubert weeks before his death. Maupassant had served in the war himself.
