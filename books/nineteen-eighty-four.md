# Summary

In 1984, Britain is "Airstrip One", a province of Oceania, a one-party state permanently at war with Eurasia or Eastasia. The Party, led by the never-seen Big Brother, watches everyone through telescreens. Winston Smith works at the Ministry of Truth rewriting old newspapers so that the past always agrees with the Party. He secretly keeps a diary, a thought crime. He begins an affair with Julia, a young woman from the Fiction Department, and they meet in a rented room above Mr. Charrington's junk shop. Believing that O'Brien, a senior Party member, belongs to the resistance led by Emmanuel Goldstein, they go to him and are given Goldstein's book, which explains how the Party works. Then Mr. Charrington turns out to be from the Thought Police, and they are arrested. In the Ministry of Love, O'Brien tortures Winston for months until he accepts that two plus two make five if the Party says so. In Room 101, faced with a cage of rats strapped to his face, Winston screams to do it to Julia instead. Released, he meets Julia once and they admit they betrayed each other. Sitting in a café, he weeps with joy at the news of a victory and realizes he loves Big Brother.

# Main characters

- **Winston Smith**: a Party member who rewrites history and rebels in secret
- **Julia**: his lover
- **O'Brien**: the Inner Party member who traps and tortures him
- **Big Brother**: the face of the Party
- **Emmanuel Goldstein**: the official enemy of the state
- **Mr. Charrington**: the shopkeeper, a Thought Police agent
- **Syme**: a linguist working on Newspeak, who is 'vaporized'
- **Parsons**: Winston's neighbor, denounced by his own daughter

# Historical & cultural context

See [[orwell-spain-and-stalinism]]. Orwell wrote it on Jura while dying of tuberculosis; it came out in June 1949. Newspeak, doublethink, Big Brother, and Room 101 all entered ordinary English. In a letter to Orwell in October 1949, Aldous Huxley predicted that the future would look more like his *Brave New World*, with power held through pleasure and conditioning rather than terror.
