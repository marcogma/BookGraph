# Summary

Dom Juan, a Spanish nobleman, seduces women and abandons them, mocks religion, and refuses to pay his debts, while his servant Sganarelle scolds him and follows him anyway. He has abandoned Doña Elvire, whom he took from a convent to marry. He tries to seduce two peasant girls, Charlotte and Mathurine, at the same time, promising marriage to both. In the forest he tells a beggar he will give him a gold coin if he swears, and the beggar refuses. He saves the life of Elvire's brother. He passes the tomb of a Commander he killed and mockingly invites the statue to dinner; the statue nods and comes. His father begs him to change, and Elvire, now a nun again, warns him. Dom Juan then announces he will become a hypocrite and pretend to be devout, since hypocrisy is the one vice society protects. When the statue returns and takes his hand, the earth opens and he is swallowed by fire. Sganarelle is left crying "Mes gages!" ("My wages!").

# Main characters

- **Dom Juan**: the libertine nobleman
- **Sganarelle**: his servant, cowardly and superstitious
- **Doña Elvire**: the wife he abandoned
- **Dom Louis**: Dom Juan's father
- **Charlotte, Mathurine, Pierrot**: the peasants in Act 2
- **The Commander**: the man Dom Juan killed, who returns as a statue

# Historical & cultural context

See [[moliere-and-the-court-of-louis-xiv]]. Molière wrote it in 1665 while *Tartuffe* was banned, and Dom Juan's speech praising hypocrisy is read as a reply to the devout party that banned it. It was withdrawn after fifteen performances and not printed in full until 1682. The Don Juan story starts with the Spanish play *El burlador de Sevilla* (around 1630), attributed to Tirso de Molina.
