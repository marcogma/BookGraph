# What happened

García Márquez was born in 1927 in Aracataca, a small town in Colombia's Caribbean banana zone, and spent his first years with his maternal grandparents. His grandfather, Colonel Nicolás Márquez, was a Liberal veteran of the Thousand Days' War (1899–1902) who spent years waiting for a pension. His grandmother Tranquilina Iguarán told stories of ghosts and omens in a flat, matter-of-fact tone, which he later said was the voice he used for *Cien años de soledad*. In 1928, near Aracataca, the Colombian army fired on striking United Fruit Company workers in the Ciénaga banana massacre.

He studied law in Bogotá, dropped out after the 1948 riots (the Bogotazo), and became a journalist, first in Cartagena and Barranquilla, then at *El Espectador* in Bogotá. His 1955 series on a shipwrecked sailor embarrassed the government of General Gustavo Rojas Pinilla, and the paper sent him to Europe. From 1961 he lived mostly in Mexico City, where he wrote *Cien años de soledad* in about eighteen months; it came out in Buenos Aires in 1967. His parents' long, opposed courtship was the starting point for *El amor en los tiempos del cólera*. He won the Nobel Prize in 1982 and died in Mexico City in 2014.

# Grounds these books

- [[one-hundred-years-of-solitude]]: Macondo is built from Aracataca, his grandparents' house, the civil wars, and the banana massacre
- [[chronicle-of-a-death-foretold]]: based on the 1951 killing of his friend Cayetano Gentile in Sucre, which he reported on decades later
- [[love-in-the-time-of-cholera]]: the courtship of his parents, Gabriel Eligio García and Luisa Santiaga Márquez, opposed by her father
- [[of-love-and-other-demons]]: the prologue describes his time as a young reporter in Cartagena in 1949
- [[relato-de-un-naufrago]]: the 1955 *El Espectador* series that ended his first stretch of journalism in Colombia
