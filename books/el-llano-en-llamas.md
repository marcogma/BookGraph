# Summary

Seventeen short stories (fifteen in the 1953 first edition) about peasants in southern Jalisco after the Revolution, told mostly in their own plain, spoken Spanish. In "Nos han dado la tierra" men walk for hours across the dry plain the government has given them as their land reform grant, where nothing will grow. In "¡Diles que no me maten!" an old man who killed a neighbor decades ago is caught and executed on the orders of the dead man's son. In "No oyes ladrar los perros" a father carries his wounded outlaw son on his back through the night toward a town, cursing him, and arrives to find the son dead. "Es que somos muy pobres" is about a family losing its cow, and with it the daughter's chance to avoid prostitution. "Luvina" describes a windswept, abandoned town. "El llano en llamas" follows a revolutionary band that turns into bandits. In "Talpa" a woman and her lover take her sick husband on pilgrimage so he will die.
