# Summary

Four years after the epidemic of white blindness, municipal elections are held in the same unnamed capital. On a rainy day almost nobody votes until late afternoon, and then about 70 percent of ballots are blank. The election is repeated, and the blank votes reach 83 percent. Nobody organized this. The government treats it as a conspiracy against democracy: it declares a state of siege, then withdraws the government, police and army from the city, and finally a bomb kills people at the metro station, which the government blames on the voters. The city keeps running peacefully on its own. Looking for a culprit, the government learns that one woman, the doctor's wife, was the only person who did not go blind four years earlier. It sends a police superintendent and two officers to build a case. The superintendent comes to believe she is innocent and refuses to frame her. Her photograph is published as the leader of the plot anyway. The superintendent resigns and tries to warn the public, and he is shot. The doctor's wife is shot on her balcony by an assassin, and the dog howls.

# Main characters

- **The doctor's wife**: the woman who could see during the epidemic, now made the scapegoat
- **The superintendent**: the police officer sent to frame her, who refuses
- **The prime minister and the interior minister**: the government that turns on the city
- **The doctor**: her husband
- **The dog of tears**: still with her

# Historical & cultural context

See [[saramago-portugal-church-and-state]]. Published in 2004 as the sequel to *Blindness*; the Portuguese titles *Ensaio sobre a Cegueira* and *Ensaio sobre a Lucidez* are a pair ("Essay on Blindness", "Essay on Lucidity"). In 2004, the year it came out, Saramago called on voters to cast blank ballots in protest, and some politicians accused the book of attacking democracy.
