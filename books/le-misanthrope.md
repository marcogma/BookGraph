# Summary

Alceste hates the flattery and white lies of polite society and insists on total honesty, while his friend Philinte argues for accepting people as they are. Yet Alceste is in love with Célimène, a young widow who is the queen of flattery and gossip, entertaining a circle of admirers with sharp portraits of absent people. Alceste tells the poet Oronte his sonnet is bad and is taken to court for it. He refuses to bribe the judges in another lawsuit and loses. Célimène's rival Arsinoé, a prude, shows Alceste a letter of Célimène's. Finally two marquis read aloud letters in which Célimène mocks each of her suitors, and all of them leave her. Alceste offers to forgive her if she will come away with him and live far from society. She refuses, and he leaves alone. Philinte and Célimène's sincere cousin Éliante will marry.

# Main characters

- **Alceste**: the man who demands complete sincerity
- **Philinte**: his friend, who accepts social convention
- **Célimène**: the witty young widow Alceste loves
- **Éliante**: her cousin, sincere and kind
- **Arsinoé**: a jealous prude
- **Oronte**: the gentleman with the bad sonnet
- **Acaste and Clitandre**: two foppish marquis

# Historical & cultural context

See [[moliere-and-the-court-of-louis-xiv]]. First performed in 1666, it was not one of Molière's popular successes at the time. Molière played Alceste, and his wife Armande Béjart played Célimène.
