# Summary

The diary of Antoine Roquentin, a thirty-year-old man living alone in the port town of Bouville, where he is writing a biography of an 18th-century adventurer, the Marquis de Rollebon. He starts feeling a strange disgust, the "Nausea", when he looks at ordinary things: a pebble, a glass of beer, his own hand. He watches the town's bourgeois on their Sunday walk and visits a museum of portraits of local notables, whom he calls "salauds" (bastards) for thinking their lives have a necessary place. His one acquaintance, the Self-Taught Man, is reading the library's books in alphabetical order. In a park, looking at the root of a chestnut tree, Roquentin understands that things simply exist, without reason or necessity: existence is "de trop" (superfluous). He gives up the biography, meets his former lover Anny in Paris and finds that she has also lost hope, and watches the Self-Taught Man be exposed and humiliated in the library for touching a boy. Before leaving Bouville, listening in a café to a jazz record, "Some of These Days", he thinks he might write a novel that could justify his existence the way the song seems to justify the singer's.

# Main characters

- **Antoine Roquentin**: the diarist
- **The Self-Taught Man (l'Autodidacte)**: a humanist reading the library alphabetically
- **Anny**: Roquentin's former lover, an actress
- **The Marquis de Rollebon**: the dead adventurer whose biography Roquentin abandons
- **Françoise**: the café owner Roquentin sleeps with

# Historical & cultural context

See [[french-existentialism]]. Sartre wrote it in the mid-1930s while teaching philosophy at a lycée in Le Havre, the model for Bouville; his working title was *Melancholia*, which the publisher Gaston Gallimard changed. Published in 1938.
