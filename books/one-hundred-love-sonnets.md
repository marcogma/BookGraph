# Summary

One hundred sonnets to Matilde Urrutia, divided into four parts: morning, afternoon, evening, and night. They are unrhymed and loose in form; Neruda called them "sonnets of wood". They describe her body, their daily life together in their houses in Isla Negra and Santiago, the Chilean landscape, and, in the night section, death and what will remain of their love after it. Sonnet XVII begins "No te amo como si fueras rosa de sal, topacio".

# Main characters

- **Matilde Urrutia**: the Chilean singer to whom the book is dedicated, Neruda's third wife
