# Summary

An adult narrator, Carlos, remembers being a schoolboy in the Colonia Roma neighborhood of Mexico City in the late 1940s, under Miguel Alemán. His classmates play "battles in the desert" in the schoolyard, copying the 1948 Arab-Israeli war. Carlitos falls in love with Mariana, the young, beautiful mother of his classmate Jim, who lives with Mariana as the mistress of a powerful politician. He skips school to tell her he loves her. She is kind to him, but the visit becomes a scandal: his conservative family sends him to confession and to a psychiatrist and moves him to another school. Years later he hears that Mariana killed herself, or that she was killed, and that Jim disappeared; when he goes to the building, the people there say they never heard of her. The neighborhood has been demolished, and the book ends with him saying that nobody remembers that time or that city.

# Main characters

- **Carlos (Carlitos)**: the narrator, as a boy and as an adult
- **Mariana**: Jim's mother, whom Carlitos loves
- **Jim**: Carlos's classmate, said to be the son of an American journalist
- **Héctor**: Carlos's older brother

# Historical & cultural context

See [[the-pri-era-mexico-1940-1982]]: the novella is set in the Alemán years, with their corruption and the Americanization of Mexico City (comics, Coca-Cola, new slang). Pacheco (1939–2014) first published it in 1980 in the newspaper *Unomásuno*, and as a book in 1981. He won the Cervantes Prize in 2009.
