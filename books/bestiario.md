# Summary

Cortázar's first story collection (1951), eight stories in which something strange enters an ordinary middle-class Buenos Aires life and is accepted without explanation. In "Casa tomada" a brother and sister living in their family house hear noises and give up rooms one by one to something unnamed, until they leave the house and lock the door. In "Carta a una señorita en París" the narrator, staying in a friend's apartment, vomits little rabbits and loses control as they multiply. In "Lejana" a rich girl in Buenos Aires feels connected to a poor woman in Budapest and ends up switching bodies with her on a bridge. In "Ómnibus" two passengers are stared at because they carry no flowers like everyone else. "Cefalea", "Circe" (a girl whose fiancés die and who fills chocolates with cockroaches), "Las puertas del cielo" (a dead woman seen at a dance hall), and "Bestiario" (a tiger loose in a country house, which kills the abusive uncle) complete the book.

# Historical & cultural context

See [[the-latin-american-boom]]. Jorge Luis Borges published "Casa tomada" in 1946 in the magazine *Los Anales de Buenos Aires*, Cortázar's first appearance in print as a fiction writer. Some readers have taken "Casa tomada" as an allegory of Peronism taking over middle-class life; Cortázar said in interviews that it came from a nightmare. *Bestiario* appeared the year he moved to Paris.
