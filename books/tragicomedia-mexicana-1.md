# Summary

A popular history of Mexico from 1940 into the 1970s, organized by presidential terms: Manuel Ávila Camacho (World War II, the end of the Cárdenas era), Miguel Alemán (industrialization, corruption, Acapulco), Adolfo Ruiz Cortines, Adolfo López Mateos (the railway workers' strike of 1958–59, the jailing of Demetrio Vallejo), and Gustavo Díaz Ordaz (the 1968 student movement and the Tlatelolco massacre), and Luis Echeverría (his attempt to win back the young with third-world rhetoric, the Halconazo of June 1971, the "dirty war" against guerrillas). Along with politics, Agustín covers popular culture: the golden age of Mexican cinema, ranchera music, bolero, the arrival of rock and roll, comic books, wrestling, and the counterculture. He writes in an informal, opinionated voice, openly critical of the PRI.

# Main characters

- **Miguel Alemán**: the modernizing, corrupt president of 1946–1952
- **Gustavo Díaz Ordaz**: president during the 1968 massacre
- **Luis Echeverría**: Díaz Ordaz's interior minister, then successor
