# What happened

Gabriela Mistral (Lucila Godoy Alcayaga, 1889–1957) was a rural schoolteacher from the Elqui Valley who first became known with her "Sonetos de la muerte" in 1914. In 1920 she was principal of the girls' secondary school in Temuco, where a teenage Neftalí Reyes, later Pablo Neruda, met her; she lent him Russian novels. In 1922 she went to Mexico at the invitation of José Vasconcelos to work on his education reforms. She spent most of her later life abroad as a Chilean consul. In 1945 she became the first Latin American writer to win the Nobel Prize.

Neruda (1904–1973) published *Veinte poemas de amor y una canción desesperada* in 1924, at nineteen, and it became one of the best-selling books of poetry in Spanish. He served as a consul in Asia and in Spain, where the Civil War and the murder of his friend Federico García Lorca in 1936 turned him political. He joined the Communist Party, was elected senator in 1945, and spent 1948–49 in hiding before escaping across the Andes on horseback. *Cien sonetos de amor* (1959) is dedicated to his third wife, Matilde Urrutia. He won the Nobel in 1971 and died on September 23, 1973, twelve days after Pinochet's coup. In 2023 an international forensic panel reported finding *Clostridium botulinum* bacteria in his remains; whether he was poisoned is still disputed.
