# What happened

In the last two decades of Queen Victoria's reign, British fiction returned to the gothic, now set in modern London and full of science, newspapers, and telegrams. Many of these books dealt with the fear that people and nations could "degenerate". The idea was spread by Max Nordau's *Degeneration* (1892) and by Darwinian worries about human beings regressing.

London in these years had the Whitechapel murders of 1888, attributed to "Jack the Ripper". A stage version of *Jekyll and Hyde* with the actor Richard Mansfield was running at the time, and some newspapers suggested Mansfield as a suspect. The 1885 Labouchère Amendment made "gross indecency" between men a crime, and Oscar Wilde was convicted under it in 1895; passages of *Dorian Gray* were read out against him at his trial. Bram Stoker knew Wilde in Dublin and married Florence Balcombe, whom Wilde had courted. Stoker spent most of his career managing the Lyceum Theatre for the actor Henry Irving.
