# Summary

A long letter Kafka wrote in November 1919, at thirty-six, to his father, Hermann Kafka, answering the father's question of why Franz was afraid of him. Kafka describes his father as a powerful, self-made, loud man who dominated the family and the table, mocked his son's friends, interests and Jewish faith, and handed out judgments he did not follow himself. He recalls being put out on the balcony at night as a small child for asking for water. He says he has always felt weak and guilty in comparison, and that his writing was an attempt to get away from his father. He blames his inability to marry (he had just broken off his engagement to Julie Wohryzek, which his father had mocked) on the same relationship. Toward the end he imagines his father's reply accusing him of being a parasite, and partly accepts it.

# Main characters

- **Hermann Kafka**: Franz's father, a self-made merchant
- **Julie Wohryzek**: the woman Kafka was engaged to in 1919
- **Ottla**: Kafka's youngest sister, who stood up to the father
