# Summary

A wealthy merchant, Vasili Andreich Brekhunov, sets out with his servant Nikita through a snowstorm to close a lucrative land deal, ignoring warnings to wait it out. They become lost and stranded overnight; Vasili Andreich, initially trying to save himself alone, ultimately lies on top of Nikita to keep him warm with his own body, sacrificing himself so his servant survives — dying having found, in his final hours, a selflessness his whole grasping life had lacked.

# Main characters

- **Vasili Andreich Brekhunov** — the merchant, self-interested for most of the story, redeemed in his final act
- **Nikita** — his servant, patient and resigned, who survives because of that sacrifice

# Notes

Vasili Andreich's death — covering Nikita with his own body in the snow — is another instance of the same Tolstoyan move as *Ivan Ilyich*: redemption that's physically witnessed and external, not just something claimed inside a character's own head.
